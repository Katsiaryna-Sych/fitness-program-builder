# Nutrition & Recovery
> Run: 20261002-gym-muscle-gain-intermediate · Step: nutrition · Owner: nutrition-planner · Attempt: 2

## Summary
The goal is muscle gain, so the direction is a small energy surplus. For a 29-year-old male (180 cm, 76 kg) who trains 4 days per week, estimated maintenance is about 2,700 kcal (Mifflin-St Jeor x 1.55). The daily target is about 2,950 kcal (range 2,850-3,000), roughly 10% above maintenance, with 137 g protein (1.8 g/kg). Aim for a slow gain of about 0.2-0.4 kg per week (0.25-0.5% of body weight). Key habits: spread protein over 4 meals of about 30-35 g, eat a carbohydrate-rich meal 1-3 hours before training, eat protein within 2 hours after training, hydrate around the Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday sessions, and sleep 7 or more hours. The calculation is an estimate: weigh yourself 3 times per week, compare 2-week averages, and adjust by about 150-250 kcal if the trend is off target. Training-day and rest-day intake are kept the same for simplicity.

## Meal Timing
- Training days are Monday (Upper A), Tuesday (Lower A), Thursday (Upper B) and Friday (Lower B). Rest days are Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday; keep the same daily calories and protein on rest days, because recovery and growth continue.
- Protein: 4 meals per day of about 0.4-0.45 g/kg, i.e. about 30-35 g each (137 g / 4 = 34 g; 0.45 x 76 = 34 g). ISSN: a 20-40 g dose every 3-4 hours suits muscle protein synthesis; the off-season review gives 0.40-0.55 g/kg per meal as optimal for bodybuilders (30-42 g).
- Before training (1-3 hours): a meal with about 1-1.5 g/kg carbohydrate (75-115 g) and 30 g protein, for example rice or oats with eggs, yogurt or chicken. Lower-fat and moderate-fibre foods digest more comfortably. The carbohydrate amount is a practical suggestion that fits the 5.7 g/kg daily total. Carbohydrate around resistance exercise supports glycogen stores (ISSN nutrient timing).
- After training (within 2 hours): a meal with 30-40 g protein and carbohydrate. ISSN nutrient timing: protein ingested immediately to 2 hours after exercise stimulates muscle protein synthesis. Total daily intake matters more than exact timing, so do not worry about a precise window.
- Before bed: 30-40 g slow-digesting protein (casein, cottage cheese or Greek yogurt) is optional and can serve as the fourth protein feeding (ISSN).
- Food quality: build meals from lean protein, whole grains, potatoes or rice, fruit, vegetables, dairy or alternatives, nuts and oils. No dietary restrictions were reported; if any apply, swap protein sources within the same gram targets.
- Weekly check: if the 2-week average weight gain is below 0.15 kg per week, add about 150-250 kcal (mostly carbohydrate); if above 0.4 kg per week, remove about 150-250 kcal. Repeat the check every 2 weeks over the 8 weeks and keep protein constant.

## Hydration
- Daily: drink regularly through the day so you start each session well hydrated. ACSM advises drinking adequate fluid in the 24 hours before exercise, including with the meal before training. Pale-yellow urine is a practical sign of good hydration (practical rule, not a cited number).
- Before training: about 5-7 ml/kg at least 4 hours before, i.e. 380-530 ml for 76 kg (ACSM). Another 250-500 ml can be sipped in the hour before a session if urine is dark.
- During training (sessions are 55-58.5 min): water is enough; start early and sip between sets, aiming to avoid losing more than 2% of body weight (1.5 kg) from sweat (ACSM). Add sodium (0.5-0.7 g per litre) only for sessions longer than 1 hour or heavy sweating.
- After training: replace losses with about 1.5 L per kg of body weight lost, together with a meal containing sodium (ACSM). Weighing before and after a session once can show the client's own sweat loss.
- Alcohol and caffeine: keep caffeine to the morning or early afternoon so it does not disturb sleep. Heavy alcohol use impairs recovery and sleep; keep it modest.

## Sleep & Recovery
- Sleep 7 or more hours per night on a regular schedule, with a consistent bedtime and wake time (AASM and Sleep Research Society consensus for adults aged 18-60).
- Rest days: Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday are rest days from lifting (as in the training program); an easy walk is optional. Do not add extra hard sessions on them.
- Deload: week 5 is a planned lower-volume week. Keep eating at the same target (or about 100-200 kcal lower if body weight rises faster than 0.4 kg per week) and use the week to catch up on sleep.
- Stress management: schedule sessions at consistent times, take 5-10 minutes of easy breathing or a walk on rest days, and limit screens before bed. If persistent fatigue, low mood, poor sleep or joint aches appear for more than a week or two, reduce training volume and speak to a doctor.
- Keep a simple log: body weight 3 times per week (morning, after the toilet), training loads and sleep hours.

## Disclaimer
- General guidance only, not medical or dietetic advice. These estimates can be off by 10% or more. Consult a doctor or registered dietitian before changing your diet if you have a medical condition (for example kidney disease, diabetes or an eating disorder), take medication, or are unsure about supplements. No conditions were reported for this client.
