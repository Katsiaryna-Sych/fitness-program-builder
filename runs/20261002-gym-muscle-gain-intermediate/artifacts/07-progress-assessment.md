# Progress & Assessment
> Run: 20261002-gym-muscle-gain-intermediate · Step: progress · Owner: progress-planner · Attempt: 3

## Summary
The client's primary goal is muscle gain over 8 weeks (4 sessions per week, upper/lower split, week 5 deload). Progress is measured with 6 baseline tests: three body measures (weekly average body weight, relaxed mid-upper arm circumference, and waist circumference as a guard against excess fat gain) and three submaximal strength tests (an 8-rep load at 2 reps in reserve) on dumbbell bench press, barbell row and barbell Romanian deadlift. The lift tests reuse the first work set of a lift the client already performs, with the normal 90 s rest and no extra rest, so they add no time to a session and every test-week session stays within the 60-minute limit (program estimates 57.5-58.5 min). No true 1-rep max, no test to failure and no maximal push-up test is used, because the gym has no rack, safety bars or spotter; every test stops with at least 1 rep in reserve. Formal checkpoints are in week 1 (baseline), week 4 (end of block 1) and week 8 (final). Week 5 is a deload and is not tested. Body weight is logged weekly, circumferences every 4 weeks, and every session is logged. A tape measure and a bathroom scale are the only items needed besides the barbell, bench and dumbbells. Both are measuring tools, not training equipment.

Note on the lift tests: the program's week 1 target is RIR 3 for calibration. In week 1 only, the first work set of tests 4-6 is taken at RIR 2, and the remaining sets drop back to RIR 3 at the same or a slightly lower load. The client's RIR estimate is most accurate near failure and improves with practice, so the first RIR 2 call may be off by a rep; keep the same lifter-judged standard at every checkpoint. The test was kept to 8 reps because the program uses 6-12 rep ranges. Test sets use the program's rest times (90 s on compound lifts); no additional rest is inserted before a test set, so every test-week session keeps the program's duration of 57.5-58.5 min and stays within 60 min.

| 8 (final) | Repeat tests 1-6 in the same order and conditions in the final week, after the week 6-7 block. | Body weight average up about 1.3-2.8 kg on baseline (about 0.2-0.4 kg per week over 7 weeks). Week 5 is a deload (sets cut about 50%, RIR 4) and no test is done in it; only weekly body weight continues.

## Adjustment Rules
All rules follow the program's double progression: once every set reaches the top of the rep range at the target RIR, add load next session (barbell +2.5 kg, dumbbells to the next pair), then rebuild reps. Week 5 stays a deload.

- If every set of a lift reaches the top of the rep range at the target RIR: add load next session (+2.5 kg barbell or the next dumbbell pair, about 1-2 kg each) and restart at the bottom of the range.
- If the reps of a lift do not increase for 2 sessions in a row (same load): keep the load, check sleep (7 hours or more) and food intake, and aim for 1 more rep on the first set. If it stalls for 3 sessions in a row: cut the load by about 10% and rebuild reps over 2 sessions.
- If performance drops on 2 sessions in a row, or joint aches or unusual fatigue persist: take an unplanned deload of 3-7 days (the program's deload rule) with about 50% of the sets and RIR 4, then resume at about 90% of the previous loads.
- If sets are being taken to RIR 0 on a barbell or dumbbell compound lift: reduce the load by 5-10% at the next session. Compound lifts stay at RIR 1 or more.
- If sets finish at 3 or more reps more than the target RIR (too easy) in two sessions in a row: add load by 2-5% (nearest dumbbell pair or 2.5 kg) rather than adding sets.
- If a session runs longer than 60 minutes (including a test-week session): keep rest at the program's times (90 s compound, 60 s isolation), never lengthen it, and drop the last set of the calf raise or core exercise; do not add sets.
- If the 2-week average body weight gain is below 0.15 kg per week: add 150-250 kcal per day in the nutrition plan and re-check after 2 weeks.
- If the 2-week average body weight gain is above 0.4 kg per week, or waist circumference is up more than 1.5 cm by week 4: remove 150-250 kcal per day from the nutrition plan and re-check after 2 weeks. Faster gains are mostly extra fat for intermediate lifters.
- If body weight and waist are on target but the arm circumference has not increased by week 8: keep the plan and re-measure in 4 weeks after any follow-on block. Changes under 1.0 cm are within measurement error.
- If 2 or more sessions are missed in one week: repeat that week rather than advancing, and keep the week 5 deload where it is. If only 1 session is missed, do it the next day and keep one rest day before the same-focus session.
- If a test set falls short (fewer than 8 clean reps, or form breaks, or sleep was under 6 hours the night before): do not count it; repeat the test at the next matching session within 7 days.
- If sharp or persistent pain appears during a test or any lift (or the client later reports a new injury or condition): stop the set, skip or swap the exercise for the session, and seek professional advice before resuming. Do not retest the painful lift until it is pain-free.

## Sources
- [Application of the Repetitions in Reserve-Based Rating of Perceived Exertion Scale for Resistance Training — Zourdos et al. 2016, Strength and Conditioning Journal (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961270/) — RIR-based RPE scale (RPE 8 = 2 RIR); suited to hypertrophy training near failure; experienced lifters are more accurate, novices should practise recording RIR
- [Reliability and Agreement of the 10-Repetition Maximum Test in Breast Cancer Survivors — PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6775190/) — repetition-maximum test procedure: warm-up at 50% of the estimated load, load changes of 5-10%; bench press ICC 0.94 (reliable for tracking); used as a procedure reference only, since the population differs (its longer rest between attempts is not adopted here; this plan uses the program's rest times)
- [A Comparison of Three Protocols for Determining Barbell Bench Press Single Repetition Maximum — PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11679921/) — direct 1RM testing needs trained spotters and supervision; repetition-based estimates under- or over-estimate 1RM, so this plan tracks the measured 8-rep load directly instead of a predicted 1RM
- [Protocol — Mid-Upper Arm Circumference — PhenX Toolkit](https://www.phenxtoolkit.org/protocols/view/21102?origin=browse) — landmarks, posture, tape placement, 0.1 cm rounding, third measurement if readings differ by more than 1.0 cm
- [Protocol — Waist Circumference — PhenX Toolkit](https://www.phenxtoolkit.org/protocols/view/21604) — iliac crest landmark, horizontal snug tape, end of normal expiration, 0.1 cm rounding, third measurement rule
- [Nutrition Recommendations for Bodybuilders in the Off-Season: A Narrative Review — Iraki et al. 2019, Sports (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/) — target weight gain of about 0.25-0.5% of body weight per week for novice/intermediate lifters with a hyper-energetic diet of about 10-20%; faster gain adds fat
- [wger exercise database](https://wger.de/en/exercise/overview/) — exercises used in the tests (ids 75, 83, 507), taken from the training program
