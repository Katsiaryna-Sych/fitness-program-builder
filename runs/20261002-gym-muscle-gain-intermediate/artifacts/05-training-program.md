# Training Program
> Run: 20261002-gym-muscle-gain-intermediate · Step: program · Owner: program-designer · Attempt: 1

## Summary
An 8-week upper/lower split on 4 days per week (Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday) for a 29-year-old intermediate male whose primary goal is muscle gain. Each major muscle group is trained twice per week. Weekly volume is 10-18 fractional hard sets per major muscle group, at moderate loads (6-15 reps) taken 1-3 reps short of failure, with 60-90 s rest. All four sessions fit within the 60-minute limit; the longest is 58.5 min. Every exercise comes from the client's exercise library and needs only a barbell loaded from the floor, a flat bench and dumbbells. No rack is needed. Progression uses double progression with a falling RIR target. Week 5 is a planned deload, and weeks 6-8 are a second, harder block. No safety screen was needed (no injuries reported) and no cardio plan was requested, so no cardio blocks are scheduled.

## Weekly Volume by Muscle Group
Counting rule: fractional set counting (Pelland 2024). A set counts as 1 for the muscle the exercise directly targets and 0.5 for a clear synergist. Romanian deadlift sets count 1 toward hamstrings and 0.5 toward glutes. Volumes are for weeks 1-4 and 6-8; the week 5 deload is about 50% of these.

| muscle group | hard sets / week | target range | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chest | 11.5 (DB bench 5 + floor press 5 = 10 direct; pullover 3 × 0.5 = 1.5) | 10-20 | Bernárdez-Vázquez 2022 (at least 10 sets/week to maximise growth); Baz-Valle 2022 (12-20 sets/week for trained men) |
| Back (lats / upper back) | 14 (barbell row 4 + pullover 3 + one-arm row 4 + lat pull 3) | 10-20 | same |
| Shoulders (deltoids) | 17 (Arnold press 3 + DB shoulder press 3 + lateral raises 6 = 12 direct; 10 horizontal-press sets × 0.5 = 5) | 10-20 | same |
| Biceps | 10 (curls 6 direct; 8 row sets × 0.5 = 4) | 10-20 | same |
| Triceps | 14 (skullcrushers 6 direct; 16 pressing sets × 0.5 = 8) | 10-20 (Baz-Valle 2022 reported more than 20 sets helped the triceps further) | same |
| Quadriceps | 14 (front squat 4 + split squat 3 + goblet squat 4 + lunges 3) | 10-20 | same |
| Hamstrings | 13 (Romanian deadlift 5 + DB Romanian deadlift 5 = 10 direct; hip thrust 6 × 0.5 = 3) | 10-20 | same |
| Glutes | 18 (hip thrust 6 direct; 14 squat/lunge sets + 10 Romanian deadlift sets = 24 × 0.5 = 12) | 10-20 | same |
| Calves | 10 (calf raise 5 + 5) | 10-20 | same |
| Core (abs / obliques) | 4 (crunches 2 + suitcase carry 2), plus bracing in all compound lifts | not a hypertrophy priority for this goal; no set target applied | Not applicable — no volume target was set for core in the cited reviews |

Push/pull balance: 16 push sets (10 horizontal + 6 vertical) vs 14 pull sets (8 rows + 6 lat-focused). The ratio is about 1.1 : 1, which counts as balanced. Lower body: 14 quad-dominant vs 10 hinge sets, plus 6 hip-thrust sets. Schoenfeld 2017 found more weekly volume gives more growth, with even 4 or fewer sets per week producing gains. Pelland 2024 found diminishing returns at higher volumes, which supports staying in the 10-20 range rather than going above it.

## Progression Plan
Base rule for every week: double progression. Work within the rep range at the target RIR. When every set of an exercise reaches the top of the range at that RIR, add load next session: barbell +2.5 kg; dumbbells to the next pair (about +1-2 kg each); carries +2 kg. Then rebuild reps from the bottom of the range. This follows the ACSM rule of a 2-10% load increase once the client can do 1-2 reps over target. Sets stay as listed so every session stays within 60 min.

| week | change | target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Calibration week. Choose loads so every set ends at RIR 3, and learn the flat-bench and from-the-floor setups. Log every load and rep count. | All prescribed sets done at RIR 3; sessions 55-58.5 min |
| 2 | Double progression begins: add 1-2 reps per set at the same load where possible. | RIR 2-3 on all lifts |
| 3 | Increase load on any exercise where all sets hit the top of the range last week (+2.5 kg barbell / next dumbbell pair). | Compound lifts RIR 2; isolation RIR 1-2 |
| 4 | Continue double progression; last week of block 1. | Compound lifts RIR 1-2; isolation RIR 1 |
| 5 | DELOAD: cut sets by about 50% (5→3, 4→2, 3→2, 2→1), keep week 4 loads or reduce them by about 10%, and stay further from failure. Same exercises and days. | RIR 4 on all lifts; sessions about 30-35 min |
| 6 | Block 2 starts: return to full sets at week 4 loads; add load wherever week 4 reached the top of the range. | Compound lifts RIR 2; isolation RIR 1 |
| 7 | Double progression; push isolation sets closer to failure. | Compound lifts RIR 1-2; isolation RIR 0-1 (last set) |
| 8 | Final week of the program: beat week 4 / week 7 loads or reps on the main lifts (DB bench, floor press, barbell row, front squat, Romanian deadlift, hip thrust). | Compound lifts RIR 1 (never 0 on barbell or dumbbell presses without a spotter); isolation RIR 0-1 |

## Deload & Recovery
- Rest-day rules: at least 1 rest day between Upper A and Upper B and between Lower A and Lower B (Mon/Thu upper, Tue/Fri lower). Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday are rest days; an easy walk is optional and not a training session. If a session is missed, do it the next day and keep at least one rest day before the next session with the same focus.
- Deload timing: week 5, after a 4-week block, in line with the Delphi consensus that deloads usually come every 4-6 weeks and last about 7 days. During a deload, reduce volume, stay further from failure, and lower intensity if needed. Take an extra unplanned deload of 3-7 days if performance drops on two sessions in a row, or if joint aches or unusual fatigue persist.
- Effort limits: no barbell or dumbbell compound lift goes to failure (RIR 1 or more), because there is no rack, safety bars or spotter. Isolation lifts may go to RIR 0 in weeks 7-8 only.
- Sleep: 7 or more hours per night, regularly, as recommended for adults aged 18-60 by the AASM/SRS consensus. Sleep supports recovery between the four sessions.
- Stop a set and seek professional advice if any lift causes sharp or persistent pain. Nutrition support for muscle gain is covered separately.
