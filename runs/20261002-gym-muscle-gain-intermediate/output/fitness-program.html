You train in an upper/lower split: Upper A, Lower A, Upper B, Lower B. Each major muscle group is trained twice per week, with 10-18 hard sets per muscle group each week. Loads are moderate (6-15 reps) and every set stops 1-3 reps short of failure, with 60-90 s rest. Weights go up by double progression: you add reps first, then load. Week 5 is a deload, and weeks 6-8 are a second, harder block.

02Your Profile & Requirements

  • You are a 29-year-old man, 180 cm and 76 kg, with about a year of irregular lifting. You describe yourself as intermediate and can train consistently.
  • Your main goal is muscle gain. You did not name secondary goals or a measurable target.
  • You train 4 days per week for no more than 60 minutes per session, over 8 weeks. You gave no preferred days, so the sessions are spaced Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday.
  • Your gym has a barbell, a bench and dumbbells only. Because there is no rack, the program uses no barbell squat, no barbell bench press and no barbell overhead press. Barbells are loaded from the floor, or rolled onto your hips for hip thrusts.
  • You reported no injuries or conditions, and there are no movements you cannot or will not do.
  • Units are metric (cm, kg). You asked for nutrition guidance, which is included below. Cardio was not requested.

03Safety Notes

No special health considerations were reported. General safety advice for this program:

  • Without a rack, safety bars or a spotter, no barbell or dumbbell compound lift goes to failure. Stay at 1 rep in reserve or more. Isolation lifts may reach 0-1 reps in reserve in weeks 7-8 only.
  • Use a neutral spine on every hinge and row, and stop the set if your lower back rounds.
  • Kick dumbbells up from your thighs for dumbbell bench press, and use the floor press as the safe pressing option when you are tired.
  • Stop a set and seek professional advice if any lift causes sharp or persistent pain. Do not return to that lift until it is pain-free.
  • If joint aches or unusual fatigue persist, or your performance drops in two sessions in a row, take an unplanned deload of 3-7 days.
  • Speak to a doctor if persistent fatigue, low mood, poor sleep or joint aches last for more than a week or two.

07Progression Plan

Base rule for every week is double progression. Work within the rep range at the target RIR. When every set of an exercise reaches the top of the range at that RIR, add load next session: barbell +2.5 kg, dumbbells to the next pair (about +1-2 kg each), carries +2 kg. Then rebuild reps from the bottom of the range. Sets stay as listed so every session stays within 60 minutes.

Meal timing

  • Protein: 4 meals per day of about 30-35 g each (137 g / 4 = 34 g). A 20-40 g dose every 3-4 hours suits muscle building.
  • Before training (1-3 hours): a meal with about 75-115 g carbohydrate (1-1.5 g/kg) and 30 g protein, for example rice or oats with eggs, yogurt or chicken. Lower-fat, moderate-fibre foods digest more comfortably.
  • After training (within 2 hours): a meal with 30-40 g protein plus carbohydrate. Total daily intake matters more than an exact window.
  • Before bed: 30-40 g slow-digesting protein (casein, cottage cheese or Greek yogurt) is optional and can serve as your fourth protein feeding.
  • Food quality: build meals from lean protein, whole grains, potatoes or rice, fruit, vegetables, dairy or alternatives, nuts and oils. If you have dietary restrictions, swap protein sources within the same gram targets.
  • Weight check: weigh yourself 3 times per week and compare 2-week averages. If the gain is below 0.15 kg per week, add about 150-250 kcal (mostly carbohydrate). If it is above 0.4 kg per week, remove about 150-250 kcal. Check every 2 weeks and keep protein constant.

Hydration

  • Drink regularly through the day so you start each session well hydrated. Pale-yellow urine is a practical sign of good hydration.
  • Before training: about 5-7 ml/kg at least 4 hours before, which is 380-530 ml for you. Sip another 250-500 ml in the hour before a session if your urine is dark.
  • During training: water is enough. Sip between sets and aim not to lose more than 2% of body weight (1.5 kg) to sweat. Add sodium (0.5-0.7 g per litre) only for sessions longer than 1 hour or heavy sweating.
  • After training: replace losses with about 1.5 L per kg of body weight lost, together with a meal containing sodium.
  • Keep caffeine to the morning or early afternoon, and keep alcohol modest because it impairs recovery and sleep.

Sleep and recovery

  • Sleep 7 or more hours per night on a regular schedule, with a consistent bedtime and wake time.
  • Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday are rest days from lifting. An easy walk is optional. Do not add extra hard sessions.
  • Deload week 5 is a planned lower-volume week. Keep eating at the same target (or about 100-200 kcal lower if body weight rises faster than 0.4 kg per week) and use the week to catch up on sleep. Deloads usually come every 4-6 weeks and last about 7 days.
  • Stress: train at consistent times, take 5-10 minutes of easy breathing or a walk on rest days, and limit screens before bed.
  • Keep a simple log of body weight 3 times per week (morning, after the toilet), training loads and sleep hours.

Adjustment rules

  • If every set of a lift reaches the top of the rep range at the target RIR: add load next session (+2.5 kg barbell or the next dumbbell pair) and restart at the bottom of the range.
  • If the reps of a lift do not increase for 2 sessions in a row at the same load: keep the load, check sleep (7 hours or more) and food intake, and aim for 1 more rep on the first set. If it stalls for 3 sessions in a row, cut the load by about 10% and rebuild reps over 2 sessions.
  • If performance drops in 2 sessions in a row, or joint aches or unusual fatigue persist: take an unplanned deload of 3-7 days with about 50% of the sets and RIR 4, then resume at about 90% of the previous loads.
  • If you are taking sets to RIR 0 on a barbell or dumbbell compound lift: reduce the load by 5-10% at the next session.
  • If sets finish 3 or more reps beyond the target RIR (too easy) in two sessions in a row: add load by 2-5% (nearest dumbbell pair or 2.5 kg) rather than adding sets.
  • If a session runs longer than 60 minutes: keep rest at the program's times (90 s compound, 60 s isolation), never lengthen it, and drop the last set of the calf raise or core exercise. Do not add sets.
  • If the 2-week average weight gain is below 0.15 kg per week: add 150-250 kcal per day and re-check after 2 weeks.
  • If the 2-week average gain is above 0.4 kg per week, or waist is up more than 1.5 cm by week 4: remove 150-250 kcal per day and re-check after 2 weeks. Faster gains are mostly extra fat for intermediate lifters.
  • If body weight and waist are on target but arm circumference has not increased by week 8: keep the plan and re-measure in 4 weeks after any follow-on block. Changes under 1.0 cm are within measurement error.
  • If 2 or more sessions are missed in one week: repeat that week rather than advancing, and keep the week 5 deload where it is. If only 1 session is missed, do it the next day.
  • If a test set falls short (fewer than 8 clean reps, form breaks, or you slept under 6 hours the night before): do not count it and repeat the test at the next matching session within 7 days.
  • If sharp or persistent pain appears during a test or any lift: stop the set, skip or swap the exercise for the session, and seek professional advice before resuming.
