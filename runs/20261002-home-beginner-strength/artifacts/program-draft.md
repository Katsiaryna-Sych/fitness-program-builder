# 12-Week Home Strength Program for Beginners
> Run: 20261002-home-beginner-strength · Step: draft · Owner: synthesizer · Attempt: 1

## Program Overview
| item | value |
|---|---|
| goal | Get stronger (strength) |
| level | Complete beginner |
| schedule | 3 days/week (Mon, Wed, Fri), ≤ 30 min per session (longest planned session 28.2 min) |
| length | 12 weeks (easier deload weeks at 6 and 12) |
| location & equipment | Home; bodyweight only, a yoga mat and one sturdy household chair |

This is a full-body program with three different sessions (A, B and C). Every session includes a squat or lunge, a push, a floor-based pull or back-extension, a hip hinge or bridge, and a core exercise. You have no weights, so you get stronger by moving to harder bodyweight variations, slower lowering and single-leg work. You never train to failure. Each set stops with a few repetitions "in reserve" (RIR), meaning you could have done that many more with good form.

Each set takes about 1 minute of work or less, and rests are short, so every session fits inside your 30-minute limit. The times shown are worst cases, from the hardest week and the hardest variation of each exercise.

## Safety Notes
No special health considerations were reported. General safety advice still applies:
- Put the chair against a wall for the box squat, chair dips and incline push-ups so it cannot slide.
- On chair dips, never lower deeper than a right angle at the elbow.
- Do not train to failure. Stop each set with good form and a few reps left.
- Stop any exercise that causes sharp or joint pain and use the easier option from the Exercise Reference. If pain lasts more than a few days, have it checked by a health professional.
- Stop any assessment test at the first sign of form breakdown, and stop the wall squat hold at once if your knees hurt.
- Never train on back-to-back days. Do not do hard training right after a heavy meal.
- See a doctor if you have any doubts about your health before starting.

## Workout Sessions
How to read the tables: "RIR" is reps in reserve. The target for each week is in the Progression Plan. Reps are for the top of each range; move to the next variation when you reach the top of the range with clean form.

How the times are worked out: work time for each set is the top-of-range reps multiplied by seconds per rep (lowering + pause + lifting, plus any hold), counted for both sides where that applies. Add the rest, 3 minutes of warm-up, 2 minutes of cool-down and 15 seconds for each change between exercises.

Tempo: weeks 1-3 use 2 seconds down and 1 second up. From week 4, use 3 seconds down and 1 second up on exercises where you have reached the top of the range. Lunges and bridges keep the 2-second lowering.

### Meal timing
- Through the day: 3-4 meals, each with a palm-sized protein portion (about 20-40 g), roughly every 3-4 hours. Total daily protein matters more than exact timing.
- Training days (Monday, Wednesday, Friday): eat a normal meal with carbohydrate and some protein 1-3 hours before the session, or a small snack (banana, yoghurt, toast) 30-60 min before if you train early or on an empty stomach. Do not train right after a heavy meal. The sessions are continuous with only 45-60 s rests, so a full stomach is uncomfortable during squats, lunges and hinges.
- After training: have a meal with about 20-40 g protein plus carbohydrate within a few hours. There is no need to rush a shake.
- Rest days: keep the same meal pattern and protein target.
- Deload weeks (6 and 12): do not cut food intake; keep eating at maintenance. The week-12 Friday retest is hard for its length, so eat a normal pre-session meal.
- Sessions are under 30 minutes, so no food or sports drink is needed during training.

### Hydration
- Daily: drink regularly. Use thirst and pale-yellow urine as a guide rather than a fixed number. Keep a water bottle at hand and choose water over sugary drinks.
- Before training: about 5-7 ml per kg body weight at least 4 hours before (for example 300-420 ml for 60 kg). If your urine is dark or scant, drink another 3-5 ml/kg about 2 hours before.
- During: sips of water between exercises are enough for sessions of 30 min or less.
- After: drink to replace sweat, with a meal containing some salt. If your body weight is lower after training, drink about 1.3-1.6 L per kg lost over the following hours.

### Rest, sleep and recovery
- Rest days: Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday. Never do sessions on back-to-back days. If a session is missed, shift the rest of the week by one day instead of doubling up. An easy walk on rest days is optional.
- Deload timing: weeks 6 and 12 (one set fewer per exercise, RIR 4). Take an extra deload week early if joint aches, unusual tiredness or falling rep counts last more than a week.
- Effort: no sets to failure. Research shows training to failure is not needed for gains, and stopping short lowers fatigue and injury risk for a beginner.
- Rest between sets: research found that rest length between sets did not change strength gains, so the short 45-60 s rests that keep you under 30 minutes are reasonable. (The evidence on muscle mass is not strong enough to say the same for muscle size.)
- Time cap: if a session ever runs over 30 min (for example, longer rests), cut the second set of the last core exercise first. Do not cut the warm-up.
- Sleep: aim for 7 or more hours a night if you are 18-60. Ages 61-64 need 7-9 hours, and 65 and over need 7-8 hours.
- Stress: poor sleep and high stress slow recovery. Use the 1-minute slow-breathing finish of each session, keep a regular bedtime, and avoid caffeine late in the day.
- Alcohol: keep intake low, especially in the evening after training, because it harms sleep and recovery.
- See a doctor or registered dietitian for unexplained fatigue, weight change without trying, pain lasting more than a few days, or any medical condition, pregnancy, medication that affects diet, or a history of disordered eating.

## Tracking & Assessments
### Baseline tests
Four tests measure your strength as strength-endurance of the legs, chest/arms and trunk. They use only the mat, the chair, a wall and a timer. Do them on the Saturday of weeks 1, 6 and 12, so a test never sits next to a hard session. Do them in this order after the standard 3-minute warm-up (Cow-cat 90 s, then 2 × 30 s Wall Squat holds). Rest 3-5 minutes between tests, test at the same time of day, and use the same chair and mat each time. The tests take about 20 minutes in total. Do not do more than these four on that day. Every test ends at the first sign of form breakdown, not at exhaustion.

| test | how to do it | what it measures |
|---|---|---|
| 30-second chair stand | Use the sturdy chair (straight back, no armrests, seat about 43 cm high) against a wall. Sit in the middle, feet flat, wrists crossed with hands on opposite shoulders. On "go", rise to full standing and sit back down for 30 s. Count full stands; a stand that is more than halfway up when 30 s ends counts. If you need your arms to stand, stop and record 0, and keep using the Box squat for 2 more weeks | Lower-body strength and endurance |
| Knee push-up max reps | On the mat, kneel with hands shoulder-width apart and back straight. Lower the chest toward the floor to the same level every time, and press to straight arms. Count clean reps; a brief pause at the top is allowed, but do not rest your body on the floor. Stop at the first rep with sagging hips or short depth. Always use the knee version at all three checkpoints. If you already do floor push-ups at week 6 or 12, do the knee test first, then record one floor push-up set after 5 min rest | Upper-body push endurance |
| Plank hold | Forearms on the mat, body in one straight line from head to toe (or knees for the knee version), head neutral, looking at the floor. Start the timer once the position is correct. Stop when your hips drop or your back cannot stay straight. Record seconds. Use the same version each time | Trunk (core) endurance |
| Wall squat hold | Feet shoulder-width apart and about 60 cm from a wall, back flat against the wall, knees and hips at about 90 degrees, knees above the ankles, arms across the chest. Time from when you reach the position until you cannot hold it. Stop at once if your knees hurt | Isometric lower-body strength-endurance |

### Adjustment rules
- Progression: if all sets of an exercise reach the top of the capped range (12 reps; 10 per leg for lunges; 8 per leg for Single Leg Glute Bridge; 8 per side for Bird Dog; 8 for Superman at a 4 s hold; or the top of the hold range) at RIR 2 or more for two sessions in a row, move to the next variation and reset reps to 6-8. Do not raise reps above the caps; they keep sessions within 30 min.
- Too easy: if your RPE is 6 or below on all exercises for two sessions in a row in weeks 3-5 or 7-11, start the 3-s lowering early (not on lunges, which stay at 2 s down) or move to the next variation. Do not add sets and do not shorten rest below 60 s (main lifts) or 45 s (holds).
- Stall: if reps do not increase for 2 weeks in a row on an exercise (not counting deload weeks), stay on the same variation, repeat the previous week's reps and check sleep and rest. If the stall lasts 3 weeks, drop to the previous easier variation for 1 week, then rebuild.
- Too hard: if you cannot finish a set with clean form at 6 reps, or your RPE is 9-10 on the first set, step down one variation (for example Push-Up to the chair incline or knee version) or drop one set from that exercise for the week.
- Fatigue: if rep counts fall for more than one week, or you feel unusually tired or have aching joints, bring forward an extra deload week (one set fewer per exercise, RIR 4).
- Pain: if an exercise causes sharp or joint pain, or pain that lasts more than a few days, stop that exercise, use the easier option from the Exercise Reference, and see a health professional if the pain continues. Do not train through it.
- Session over 30 min: cut the second set of the last core exercise first; never cut the warm-up.
- Missed sessions: if one session is missed, shift the rest of the week by one day. If a full week is missed, repeat the previous week's loads. If 2 or more weeks are missed, restart at week 1 targets and retest.
- Checkpoint failure: if fewer than 3 of the 4 tests improved at week 6, check attendance first. If you did fewer than 80% of sessions, continue unchanged and focus on attendance. If you did 80% or more, keep the current variations for weeks 7-8 (so the Single Leg Glute Bridge and Prisoner Squat swaps wait until the criteria are met) and check sleep, food and rest. Do not lengthen rests between sets.
- Test safety: if a test cannot be done with clean form or causes pain, stop, record the value reached and do not repeat it that day. Replace it with an easier version (for example the knee plank) and keep that version for all later checkpoints.
