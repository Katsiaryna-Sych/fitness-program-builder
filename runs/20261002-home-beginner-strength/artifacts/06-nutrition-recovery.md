## Meal Timing
- Through the day: 3-4 meals, each with a palm-sized protein portion (about 20-40 g), spaced roughly every 3-4 hours. Total daily protein matters more than exact timing (ISSN).
- Training days (Monday, Wednesday, Friday): eat a normal meal with carbohydrate and some protein 1-3 hours before the session, or a small snack (banana, yoghurt, toast) about 30-60 min before if training early or on an empty stomach. Do not train right after a heavy meal; the sessions are continuous with only 45-60 s rests, so a full stomach is uncomfortable during squats, lunges and hinges.
- After training: have a meal containing about 20-40 g protein plus carbohydrate within a few hours. ISSN notes benefit comes from either pre- or post-workout protein and that individual tolerance decides, so there is no need to rush a shake.
- Rest days (Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, Sunday): keep the same meal pattern and protein target.
- Deload weeks (6 and 12): sessions drop to about 18-19 min with fewer sets, but do not cut food intake; keep eating at maintenance. The week-12 Friday retest (20.7 min) is hard for its length, so eat a normal pre-session meal.
- Sessions are under 30 min so no in-session food or sports drink is needed.

## Hydration
- Daily: drink regularly through the day; needs vary with body size, activity and heat, so use thirst and pale-yellow urine as a guide rather than a fixed number. Keep a water bottle at hand and choose water over sugary drinks.
- Before a training session (Mon, Wed, Fri): NSCA suggests about 5-7 ml per kg body weight at least 4 hours before (for example 300-420 ml for 60 kg), and if urine is dark or scant, another 3-5 ml/kg about 2 hours before.
- During: with 45-60 s rests, sips of water between exercises are enough for sessions of 30 min or less; sports drinks matter only for exercise beyond 60-90 min.
- After: drink to replace sweat with a meal containing some salt; heavy sweating in a hot room calls for more fluid. If body weight is lower after training, NSCA suggests 600-720 ml per pound lost (about 1.3-1.6 L per kg lost) over the following hours.

## Sleep & Recovery
- Sleep: 7 or more hours per night for adults 18-60 (7-9 h for ages 61-64, 7-8 h for 65+), consistent with 05-training-program.md (CDC).
- Rest days: Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday are rest days; an easy walk is optional. Never train on back-to-back days; shift the week instead of doubling up.
- Deload: weeks 6 and 12 reduce volume; an extra easy week is advisable if aches, unusual tiredness or falling reps persist more than a week.
- Stress: poor sleep and high stress slow recovery; use the 1-min slow-breathing finish of each session, keep a regular bedtime, and avoid caffeine late in the day (general guidance).
- Alcohol: keep intake low, especially in the evening after training, as it harms sleep and recovery (general guidance).
- See a doctor or registered dietitian for unexplained fatigue, weight change without trying, pain lasting more than a few days, or any medical condition.
