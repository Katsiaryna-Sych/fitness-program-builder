# Training Program
> Run: 20261006-beginner-marathon-4-weeks · Step: program · Owner: program-designer · Attempt: 2

## Summary
A 4-week, 2-day full-body running program: Session A on Monday, Session B on Friday. Every session takes 20 minutes or less, which is the client's limit. Each session has three parts:
1. The run/walk cardio block, copied exactly from the cardio plan. It lasts 12, 13, 14 and 15 min in weeks 1 to 4, and its opening walk is the warm-up.
2. A short bodyweight strength block for running support: 3 exercises, 1 set each, about 45 s of work and 30 s rest.
3. 1 to 2 minutes of stretching.

The primary goal (run a marathon, 42.2 km) and the fixed limits (4 weeks, 2 days, 20 minutes) stay exactly as the client asked.

To be honest about what these limits allow: 2 sessions of at most 20 minutes come to about 40 minutes of training per week. That is enough to start the habit, build a first aerobic base and add basic leg and core strength. It is not enough to prepare a complete beginner for 42.2 km.
- Typical marathon plans run 16 to 20 weeks, and runners usually build 3 to 6 months of running four times per week before starting one (Runner's World).
- Jogging time per session rises from 4 to 6, 8 and 9 minutes. The longest continuous jog is 3 minutes, in week 4.
- Total distance over the 4 weeks is about 12 to 20 km.

This program does not include a marathon attempt, and we strongly advise against trying to run 42.2 km at the end of week 4. Please check with a doctor before any long-distance event. A sensible next step after week 4 is a longer base-building phase, then a full marathon block.

## Weekly Volume by Muscle Group
| muscle group | hard sets / week | target range | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quads | 2 (squat in A, reverse lunge in B), plus running | 2 strength exposures per week (novice frequency 2 to 3 days per week); within the lowest studied volume band of fewer than 5 sets per muscle per week | ACSM 2009; Schoenfeld et al. 2017 |
| Glutes | 2 (reverse lunge in B, single-leg bridge in B), plus squat in A as a secondary muscle | 2 exposures per week; fewer than 5 sets per week | ACSM 2009; Schoenfeld et al. 2017 |
| Hamstrings | 2 (good morning in A; single-leg bridge in B as a secondary muscle) | 2 exposures per week; fewer than 5 sets per week | ACSM 2009; Schoenfeld et al. 2017 |
| Core / abs | 2 (plank in A, bird dog in B) | 2 exposures per week; fewer than 5 sets per week | ACSM 2009; Runner's World (core, hip and glute strength for runners) |
| Calves | 0 direct sets; worked by every run/walk block | not targeted directly (running is the stimulus) | Blagrove et al. 2018 (strength work adds to running, it does not replace it) |
| Upper-body push / pull | 0 / 0 (balanced at zero) | not targeted in this program | WHO includes muscle-strengthening in its adult guidance. The 20-minute limit and the run-focused exercise library leave no room for it, which is a stated limitation |

**Balance:** The lower-body front chain (squat, lunge) and back chain (hinge, bridge) get 2 sets each. Core anti-extension (plank) and anti-rotation (bird dog) get 1 set each. Upper-body push and pull are both 0, so they are equal.

**Why volume is low:** Schoenfeld et al. 2017 found a graded dose-response, so more weekly sets give more muscle gain. Their volume bands were fewer than 5, 5 to 9, and 10 or more sets per muscle per week. This program deliberately stays in the lowest band for three reasons:
- The goal is endurance, not muscle size.
- There are only 2 to 3 days between sessions.
- Higher volumes do not fit into 20 minutes alongside the running.

The 2 strength exposures per week match the 2 to 3 sessions Blagrove et al. 2018 found beneficial for distance runners.

## Progression Plan
| week | change | target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline. Cardio block 12 min: 3 min walk + 4 x (1 min jog + 1 min walk) + 1 min walk (4 min jogging). Strength: 1 set per exercise. Squat and good morning 12 reps; lunge, bridge and bird dog 6 per side; plank 20 s | Every jog interval at RPE 5 to 6 (you can talk). Every strength set at RIR 2 to 3 with clean form |
| 2 | Cardio block 13 min: 3 min walk + 4 x (1.5 min jog + 1 min walk) (6 min jogging; +8% weekly cardio minutes). Strength (double progression): +2 reps (14; 7 per side) and plank 25 s, but only if every week 1 set ended at RIR 2 or more | Same easy effort over the longer jogs. No soreness that lasts more than 48 h |
| 3 | Cardio block 14 min: 2 min walk + 4 x (2 min jog + 1 min walk) (8 min jogging; +8%). Strength: +2 reps (16; 8 per side), plank 30 s. The rep count now passes 15, the muscular-endurance range (ACSM 2009) | Keep the walk breaks. Stay at or below RPE 6 while jogging |
| 4 | Cardio block 15 min: 3 min walk + 3 x (3 min jog + 1 min walk) (9 min jogging; +7%). Strength: 18 to 20 reps (9 to 10 per side), plank 35 s. Cool-down shortened to 1 min to stay within 20 min. For bodyweight, ACSM's "1 to 2 reps over target" load rule means: if all sets reach the top of the range at RIR 2 or more, the next program switches to the harder option in Substitutions (Prisoner Squat, Single Leg RDL, Side Plank) | Checkpoint: write down the longest stretch you can jog while still able to talk (planned: 3 min), plus the reps for each set. No marathon attempt in this program |

**Deload:** none scheduled. The program is 4 weeks long (under 6), and weekly cardio rises only 7 to 8 percent (under the roughly 10 percent limit from Runner's World). If a week feels too hard, repeat it instead of progressing (see Deload & Recovery).

## Deload & Recovery
- **Rest days:** Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday. Thursday and Sunday are full rest. On the other rest days an optional easy walk of up to 30 minutes is fine (WHO: all physical activity counts; easy walking adds no running load). This keeps at least one full day off running each week, as Runner's World advises.
- **Deload timing:** there is no planned deload in a 4-week program. Repeat the previous week's cardio pattern and reps instead of progressing if either of these happens:
  - pain beyond normal muscle soreness
  - soreness lasting more than 48 h

  Runner's World says to scale back and take an extra rest day if you feel pain.
- **Intensity guard:** if you cannot say more than a few words without pausing for breath, the effort is vigorous (CDC RPE 7 to 8). Walk until your breathing settles.
- **Sleep:** get plenty of sleep and keep time for recovery to avoid overtraining and burnout (Runner's World).
- **Medical note:** you are a complete beginner aiming for a marathon, so see a doctor before increasing running volume or entering any long event. Stop and get medical advice if you have chest pain, dizziness or joint pain.
- **After week 4:** keep training 2 or more times per week. Build up gradually to running 30 minutes without stopping and to a comfortable 10 km before starting a 16 to 20-week marathon plan (Runner's World).
