# Progress & Assessment
> Run: 20261006-beginner-marathon-4-weeks · Step: progress · Owner: progress-planner · Attempt: 1

## Summary
This plan tracks the primary goal (endurance, building toward a marathon) with 4 simple tests that need no equipment and are safe for a complete beginner. The tests are repeated at three checkpoints: week 1 (baseline), week 2 (mid-program) and week 4 (final).
- One test (longest talkable jog) is read from the normal Monday and Friday sessions and costs no extra time. This keeps the 20-minute session limit untouched.
- The other three tests (resting pulse, 6-minute brisk walk, plank hold) are done on an optional rest day (Tuesday in weeks 1 and 2, Saturday in week 4). They are not training sessions and take about 10 minutes in total.
- All tests are submaximal. Nobody is asked to go "all out": walking is at a talkable pace, the plank is capped at 60 s, and you stop at the first sign of form breaking down, pain or dizziness.
- Honest expectation: 4 weeks at 2 sessions per week measures the start of an aerobic base. These tests will show whether you are improving, but they cannot show readiness for 42.2 km. The program itself advises against a marathon attempt at the end of week 4.

## Baseline Tests
| test | protocol | what it measures | safe for client? | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resting pulse | In the morning, before getting up, caffeine or exercise, lie or sit calmly for 3 to 5 minutes. Place two fingers on the inner wrist and count beats for 60 s. Repeat on 3 mornings that week and record the average. Needs a clock or phone timer | Resting heart rate, a general marker of aerobic adaptation (a gradual drop over weeks is expected, but not guaranteed in 4 weeks) | Yes. Passive, no effort. If the pulse is above 100 or below 60 at rest and you feel unwell, see a doctor | [AHA — All About Heart Rate (Pulse)](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/the-facts-about-high-blood-pressure/all-about-heart-rate-pulse) |
| 6-minute brisk walk (adapted, talkable pace) | Optional rest day, no warm-up run. Rest seated 10 min. Walk a flat, repeatable route (same loop or a straight path with a start landmark) for 6 min at a brisk pace where you can still talk but not sing (RPE 5 to 6). Slowing down or stopping to rest is allowed; the timer keeps running. Do not jog. Record the distance (landmarks, map app or step count) and RPE at the end | Aerobic walking capacity and pacing. The standard test asks for "as far as possible"; this version caps effort at moderate for a beginner | Yes, after adaptation. The original instruction "walk as far as possible" can lead to near-maximal effort, so the pace cap is applied. Stop for chest pain, dizziness or severe breathlessness | [6 Minute Walk Test (6-MWT) — TREK Education](https://exercise.trekeducation.org/assessment/aerobic-endurance-testing/6-minute-walk-test/); [Measuring Physical Activity Intensity — CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html) |
| Longest talkable jog (in-session) | During the normal Monday session, note the longest single jog interval you completed while still able to talk but not sing (CDC talk test). Planned values: 1 min (week 1), 1.5 min (week 2), 2 min (week 3), 3 min (week 4). Record whether each interval was completed, and the RPE | Running-specific endurance at moderate effort; the best fit to the primary goal | Yes. Effort is limited to moderate by the talk test, with walk breaks built in. Never a maximal run or a time trial for a beginner | [Measuring Physical Activity Intensity — CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html) |
| Plank hold, capped at 60 s | Optional rest day. Forearms on the floor, elbows under shoulders, body in a straight line, feet hip-width apart. Hold until your hips drop or your back sags, or until 60 s, whichever comes first. Do it on your knees if a full plank is too hard and note which version you used (use the same version at every checkpoint) | Core endurance that supports running posture | Yes, with the cap. A "hold as long as possible" test is replaced by a 60 s cap with a form-based stop, so a beginner never grinds to failure | [The Plank Fitness Test — Topend Sports](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/plank.htm) |

## Checkpoints
| week | assessment | pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (baseline) | Resting pulse (3-morning average) and 6-minute brisk walk on the Tuesday after Session A, plank hold on the same day, longest talkable jog logged on Monday and Friday | Baseline recorded for all four tests. Pass for the week: both sessions completed, every jog interval at RPE 5 to 6, no pain |
| 2 (mid-program) | Same three rest-day tests on Tuesday after Session A (the test is done as a repeat of week 1: same route, same time of day, same plank version). Longest talkable jog from the Monday and Friday sessions | Both week-2 sessions completed; longest talkable jog at least 1.5 min (planned); 6-minute walk distance at least equal to baseline at the same or lower RPE; plank at least equal to baseline; resting pulse not higher than baseline by more than 5 beats |
| 4 (final) | Same three rest-day tests on the Saturday after Session B; longest talkable jog from Friday of week 4 | Longest talkable jog 3 min (planned) while able to talk; 6-minute walk distance above baseline (a gain of about 5 to 10 percent is a reasonable expectation, not a guarantee) or equal at lower RPE; plank above baseline, up to the 60 s cap; resting pulse equal to or lower than baseline. All 8 sessions completed or repeated per the adjustment rules |

| metric | how to record | frequency |
|---|---|---|

| Longest talkable jog (min) | Training log, from the run/walk block | Every session; formal checkpoint in weeks 1, 2 and 4 |
| Jog RPE (0 to 10) and talk test pass or fail | Training log: rate the last jog interval | Every session |
| Strength reps at RIR 2 to 3 (squat, good morning, lunge, bridge, bird dog) and plank seconds | Training log: reps or seconds achieved | Every session |
| 6-minute brisk walk distance and end RPE | Distance in meters (or steps) and RPE on the same route | Weeks 1, 2 and 4 |
| Resting pulse (3-morning average) | Beats per minute, counted for 60 s | Weeks 1, 2 and 4 |
| Soreness and pain | Note "none / normal soreness / lasts more than 48 h / pain" | Every session |

## Adjustment Rules
These rules match the program's progression rule (progress only if the previous week ended at RIR 2 or more and with no lasting soreness; otherwise repeat the week).
- If any jog interval cannot be done while able to talk (RPE 7 or above): walk until breathing settles, finish the session at walking pace for the remaining intervals, and repeat the same week's cardio pattern next session instead of progressing.
- If soreness lasts more than 48 h or the week felt too hard: repeat the same week once before moving on. The repeat counts as the same week, so the planned weeks are not removed from the 4-week program; note in the log that progression was held.
- If a checkpoint result is worse than baseline (shorter walk distance, shorter plank or pulse higher by more than 5 beats): first check sleep, illness and the time of day, and repeat the test one week later. Do not add extra training to "make up" for it, because the 2-day, 20-minute limit is fixed.
- If all strength sets in the week finished at RIR 2 or more and all jogs were at RPE 5 to 6: progress to the next week as written in the program.
- If the plank reaches 60 s with good form at two checkpoints in a row: the cap stays at 60 s and the strength block moves to the harder option listed in the program (Side Plank) in the next program, not in this one.
- If pain (not normal muscle soreness), chest pain, dizziness or a feeling of an irregular heartbeat appears during a test or session: stop at once, do not repeat the test and see a doctor before continuing.
- If a session is missed: do not double up. Resume with the missed week's session on the next planned day.
- At week 4: the results are for deciding the next step, not for starting a marathon. The marathon itself is not attempted in this program. Use the final checkpoint to choose a longer base-building phase before any marathon block.

## Sources
- [All About Heart Rate (Pulse) — American Heart Association](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/the-facts-about-high-blood-pressure/all-about-heart-rate-pulse) — how to measure resting pulse (count beats for 60 s); normal resting range 60 to 100 bpm
- [Measuring Physical Activity Intensity — CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html) — talk test (moderate: can talk but not sing; vigorous: only a few words per breath) and RPE 5 to 6 as moderate
- [6 Minute Walk Test (6-MWT) — TREK Education](https://exercise.trekeducation.org/assessment/aerobic-endurance-testing/6-minute-walk-test/) — self-paced 6-minute walk, rest allowed with the timer running, no warm-up, healthy adults typically 500 to 630 m
- [The Plank Fitness Test — Topend Sports](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/plank.htm) — plank test setup and form-based termination (hips lower or back sags)
