## Summary
Conditions assessed: mild patellofemoral knee pain (doctor-diagnosed, client reports medical clearance for exercise), plus risk factors age 52, body weight 84 kg (BMI about 29.8) and beginner status with walking-only background. Overall risk level: moderate, manageable with pain-guided exercise. Medical clearance is already reported by the client; if pain does not improve after 6 weeks of consistent exercise, or any exercise causes new or clearly worse pain, she should see her doctor or a physiotherapist. The sources recommend adjusting, not eliminating, knee-loading movements, so the contraindications below are deliberately limited to a small set of high-risk or high-impact items (5 rows). Running progression for the 5 km goal must be gradual and pain-capped. Update in this attempt: the week-9 and week-10 Day C runs may last up to about 60 minutes in total (client-approved exception: 5 min walking warm-up + up to about 50 min continuous jog + 5 min walking cool-down); the longer continuous runs are covered by the limits below (easy conversational pace, pain cap unchanged, the 10% weekly running-volume rule, walk-break rule, no consecutive running days). The scope of the 10% rule (run/walk phase versus continuous running) is now stated explicitly in Load & Intensity Limits. All other sessions stay within 45 minutes. This document does not diagnose and is not medical advice.

## Contraindicated Movements

| Deep loaded knee flexion — full-depth goblet/dumbbell squats, deep lunges, sissy squats, deep Bulgarian split squats — taken into pain greater than 5/10 | Patellofemoral pain | Squatting and stair-type loading are the main aggravators; closed-chain work is useful but must stay within the pain rule, so depth is limited to the pain-free range | [Right Decisions NHS Scotland](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/ggc-msk-index/knee/patellofemoral-joint-pain/); [NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-patellofemoral-knee-pain/) |

## Safe Alternatives
| instead of | use | notes |
|---|---|---|
| Jump squats / jumping lunges | Bodyweight or dumbbell box squat to a chair, glute bridge, banded lateral walk, marching | Single-foot-contact or non-impact; hip-focused work is supported for patellofemoral pain |
| Deep dumbbell squat or deep lunge | Partial-range squat to a chair or reverse lunge within pain-free range; slow lowering (3 s) | Stay at pain 0-3/10 where possible, never above 5/10; closed-chain work is supported when tolerated |
| Fast step-ups / step-downs on a high step | Low step, slow tempo, handrail or wall support | Reduce height, reps or speed if pain rises |
| Continuous running early on | Run/walk intervals on flat routes, cadence slightly higher (7.5-10%), "run softly" | Progress only when pain stays at or below 2/10 |
| Pushing a long run at speed | Steady, comfortable pace with walking bookends; walk breaks if pain nears 2/10 | The 5 km goal is about stamina, not speed; repeating a week is acceptable |
| Impact cardio for fat loss | Brisk walking, cycling-style or low-impact intervals with no jumping | Keeps weekly aerobic volume without extra joint impact |
| Knee-dominant only leg work | Combined hip (abductors, external rotators, glutes) and quadriceps strengthening with bands | Combination programmes reduce pain more than knee-only work |

## Load & Intensity Limits
- Pain rule during strength exercise: keep pain at 0-3/10 ideally and never above 5/10; if it rises, reduce range, reps, load or speed. Pain should settle to baseline within 30 minutes and not be worse the next morning. ([NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-patellofemoral-knee-pain/); [NHS Lanarkshire](https://www.nhslanarkshire.scot.nhs.uk/services/physiotherapy-msk/patellofemoral-pain-syndrome/))
- Pain rule during running: self-adjust to keep pain at 2/10 or less; progress only when symptoms are controlled. ([PMC7740062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7740062/))
- Scope of the 10% weekly rule (explicit): "weekly running volume" means total jogging/running minutes in the week, walking excluded. (a) Run/walk interval phase (every jog block 5 min or less): the 10% rule does NOT apply, because the client starts from a walking-only baseline and the early jog volume is small; the NHS beginner plan also builds faster than 10% per week in this phase. Instead apply these limits: flat terrain, pain 2/10 or less, change only ONE variable per week (either jog block length or number of repeats, not both), no consecutive running days, and repeat or step back a week if pain exceeds 2/10 or is worse the next morning. (b) Continuous running phase (starts the first week in which any jog block exceeds 5 min): the 10% rule APPLIES from that week on. The first week of continuous running sets the baseline, and each later week's total running minutes must be no more than 110% of the previous week's total running minutes (increases above 30% are linked with more injury). A week with lower running volume (repeat or step-back week) resets the baseline to that lower week. (NHS plan: [NHS Couch to 5K plan](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/); 10% vs 30%: [PMC7740062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7740062/); one-variable-at-a-time: [PMC8365734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8365734/). The split between phases is the analyst's conservative interpretation of these sources; the sources do not define it exactly.)
- Running progression: in the early weeks use run/walk intervals with flat terrain; if pain exceeds 2/10, repeat or step back a week; once continuous running begins, apply the 10% rule above. ([PMC7740062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7740062/))
- Cadence option: raise step rate 7.5-10% to lower patellofemoral joint forces. ([PMC7740062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7740062/))
- Resistance training: 2-3 non-consecutive days per week; start with 1 set of 8-12 repetitions at low intensity, progress to 2-3 sets; RPE cap 7/10 in weeks 1-4 and RPE 8/10 maximum afterwards (conservative analyst limit, consistent with the beginner "start low and go slow" guidance). ([CCJM](https://www.ccjm.org/content/83/2/141))
- Do not combine more than one new stressor (new impact, new load) in the same week; increase exercise volume gradually by duration, frequency or intensity, one at a time. ([EASO exercise recommendations, PMC8365734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8365734/))
- Aerobic volume reference for weight loss: about 150-200 min per week at moderate intensity or above, built up gradually from her current walking. ([PMC8365734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8365734/))
- Impact days: do not run on consecutive days; at least one rest day between running sessions. This matches the NHS beginner plan, which asks for at least one rest day between each of the three weekly runs. ([NHS Couch to 5K plan](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/))
- Longer continuous runs (week 9 and week 10 Day C, session up to about 60 min total): the 60-minute exception is a total session length of 5 min walking warm-up + up to about 50 min continuous jog + 5 min walking cool-down, so continuous running time in these sessions must not exceed about 50 min. It remains subject to the 10% rule: that week's total running minutes (this run plus the other running sessions) must be no more than 110% of the previous week's total running minutes, so the cardio plan must build the preceding weeks' running volume so that a 50-minute continuous run is reachable within this limit; if it is not, shorten the long run rather than breaking the rule. The NHS beginner plan reaches 5 km-level running with three 30-minute continuous runs plus 5-minute walk bookends (about 40 minutes), so up to 50 minutes of continuous jogging is well beyond the minimum needed for 5 km and is a ceiling, not a target. ([NHS Couch to 5K plan](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/); [PMC7740062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7740062/))
- Pace and effort on long runs: steady, comfortable pace; the NHS plan stresses it is not about speed and to stop if something does not feel right. Use an easy, conversational effort (RPE 5-6/10, analyst limit; no RPE above 7/10), flat route, no hills or speed work, and keep the 7.5-10% higher cadence option. ([NHS Couch to 5K plan](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/); [PMC7740062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7740062/))
- Long-run entry condition: only attempt continuous running of 20 min or more when the previous week's runs were completed with pain at 2/10 or less and no worse next morning; otherwise repeat the previous week. In week 9 and week 10 the long run may not be combined with any other new stressor (no added resistance volume, no new surface, no pace increase) in the same week. ([PMC7740062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7740062/); [PMC8365734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8365734/))
- During the long run (walk-break rule): if pain reaches 3/10 or more, switch to walking for 2-3 minutes and resume only if pain returns to 2/10 or less; if it does not settle, finish the session walking. The 60-minute total and 50-minute continuous-jog caps are hard ceilings, not targets. ([PMC7740062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7740062/))
- Every other session (including all resistance days and all earlier running days) stays at 45 minutes maximum.

## Warning Signs
- Stop the session and seek medical advice for: chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, dizziness or fainting, palpitations.
- Stop the exercise if it causes new pain, sharp pain, or pain above 5/10 during strength work (above 2/10 during running, with the walk-break rule above for long runs); do not continue through it.
- Seek advice if knee swelling, locking, giving way, or night pain appears, or if pain is worse the next morning or does not return to baseline within 30 minutes.
- Seek advice from a doctor or physiotherapist if there is no improvement after 6 weeks of consistent exercise, or if red flags such as trauma, fever or hot swollen joint, or calf swelling occur (possible clot).
- Medical clearance: the client reports her doctor has cleared her for exercise; the coordinator should still advise confirming this before starting running intervals, and mentioning the plan for a longer continuous run (up to about 50 min of jogging, 60 min total, in weeks 9-10).
