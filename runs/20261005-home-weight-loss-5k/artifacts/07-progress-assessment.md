# Progress & Assessment
> Run: 20261005-home-weight-loss-5k · Step: progress · Owner: progress-planner · Attempt: 4

## Summary
Progress is measured against the two goals: fat loss (primary) and running 5 km without stopping (secondary). Five baseline tests are used, all doable at home with no equipment beyond a tape measure, a chair, a timer and a flat route: waist circumference (with body weight), a 6-minute walk, a 30-second chair stand, a forearm plank hold, and the Saturday cardio session (a walk-based baseline in week 1, run capacity from week 5). They are done at baseline in week 1, at the mid-program checkpoint in week 5 and at the final checkpoint in week 10. A short first-jog safety check is made in week 3 and a run readiness check in week 9. Body weight and waist are also logged weekly, and every session is logged with RPE and a knee pain score. The week 9 pace check from the training program (about 4.2 km in 42 min) is an extra gate before the goal run.

The program has 3 sessions per week (Mon Session A, Wed Session B, Sat Session C). Mon and Wed sessions run up to 43.6 min (32.8 min in weeks 1 and 10), so they are close to the 45 min limit. Weeks 1-2 are walk-only and there is no jogging in week 1, so the week-1 run-capacity baseline is a walk, not a run. It uses the Saturday Day C session exactly as written in the cardio plan: week 1 = knee activation (2 sets of 10 reps per leg) plus a 20 min brisk walk (24 min session); week 2 = 25 min brisk walk (29 min). Jogging starts in week 3, and the run test is first repeated in the same Saturday session in week 5, the first continuous-running week: 5 min walk, 8 min jog, 2 min walk, 8 min jog, 2 min walk, 5 min jog, 3 min walk (33 min block, 37 min session with knee activation; 21 jog min, longest jog 8 min). Later references are week 9 = 5 + 42 + 5 (52 min) and week 10 = 5 + up to 50 + 5 (up to 60 min). Running is not tested before week 3, because she has no jogging baseline and testing run capacity at the very start would be a new impact stressor on a beginner with knee pain.

Reference values from the cardio plan, weeks 1 to 10. Weekly jog total (Mon + Wed + Sat, min): 0, 0, 18, 30, 33, 36, 39, 42, 46, 50. Longest unbroken jog (min): 0, 0, 2, 3, 8, 18, 26, 34, 42, 50. Saturday session length (min): 24, 29, 35, 41, 37, 44, 43, 44, 52, 60. Mon/Wed session length (min): 32.8, 43.6, 41.6, then 43.6 in weeks 4-9 and 32.8 in week 10. In weeks 1-2 and in the Mon/Wed sessions of week 10 the cardio block is an easy brisk walk (15 min).

Safety design: the client has mild patellofemoral knee pain and is a beginner, so no test is maximal. The chair stand and plank are pain-limited and capped, running is never tested for speed, and the 6-minute walk is self-paced. Jumping tests, 1-5 rep-max tests, deep squat tests and timed runs were rejected as unsafe. A run-capacity test in week 1 was also rejected, because the plan has no jogging then. Tests are done on the non-training days (Tuesday and Thursday) or before a session, so no training session goes over its time limit; only the waist and weight reading is taken on a Monday, before the warm-up, and it is skipped to the Tuesday if it would push that session past 45 min. The pass criteria below are targets set by this plan relative to the client's own baseline, not population norms. Nothing here is medical advice; if pain does not improve after 6 weeks of consistent exercise, or swelling, locking or giving way appears, she should see her doctor or physiotherapist.

## Adjustment Rules
- Pain, walk-only phase (weeks 1-2): if walking pain is above 3/10, or the knee is worse the next morning, repeat that week before going on. Do not start jogging in week 3 unless the week 1-2 walks were done at pain 2/10 or less and no worse the next morning (the program's long-run entry logic, applied to the first jog).
- Pain exactly 3/10 while walking (weeks 1-2, and the Mon/Wed walks in week 10): slow to an easy pace for 2-3 min and keep walking only if the pain falls to 2/10 or less; otherwise finish the walk at a very easy pace and add no time. The session counts as done, but the week is a hold: do not progress (week 1 to 2, or the week 3 jogging start) and repeat that week once. If the knee is also worse the next morning, treat it as above 3/10. Jogging starts only after a full week of walks at 2/10 or less. If the same week reaches 3/10 twice, stop progressing and see her doctor or physiotherapist.
- Pain, running: if running pain is above 2/10, or the knee is worse the next morning, repeat the previous week's run plan and keep strength loads unchanged (the program's repeat rule). If pain goes to 3/10 during a jog, walk 2-3 min and resume only if it falls to 2/10 or less; otherwise finish the session walking.
- Pain, strength: if strength pain goes above 5/10 in any exercise, stop that exercise, drop to the easier option given in the program (for example swap reverse lunges for the box squat; for the incline push-up use a higher hand position) and repeat the previous week's loads. If the pain is still above 3/10 after 2 weeks, or does not improve after 6 weeks of consistent exercise, or there is swelling, locking or giving way, stop progressing and see her doctor or physiotherapist.
- Test pain: if a baseline test (chair stand or plank) causes knee pain above 3/10, record the score at the point of stopping, use the cushion or toes version, and keep that version for all later checkpoints.
- Too hard: if a session is rated RPE 8 or higher in weeks 1-5 (the program cap is 7), or RPE 9 or higher in weeks 6-9 (cap 8), repeat the previous week's loads. Two such sessions in a row means the next week is a repeat week with 1 set per exercise.
- Too easy: if all sets are finished at the top of the rep range at RPE 5 or less in two sessions in a row, take the next load step from the program's double progression (about 2-10%, about 1 kg per hand, a stronger band or a lower hand height for the push-up). From week 4 this applies to the upper body only (floor press, one-arm row, band row, shoulder press, pull-aparts, push-up); lower-body steps start from week 6, as scheduled. Do not add extra sessions, extra repeats, longer jog blocks or running minutes outside the cardio plan.
- At the limit (weeks 8-9): an exercise already at 10 kg per hand or the strongest band stays at the top of its rep range (10-12; bands 12-15) with the same tempo. Do not add reps, slow the lowering or add sets, because Mon/Wed sessions are already up to 43.6 min against the 45 min limit. Log it as "at limit" and take no further step.
- Weekly volume: the program target is 4-9 hard sets per muscle group per week (full volume weeks 2-9; weeks 1 and 10 are lower by design). If a group counts below 4 in weeks 2-9, repeat the missed exercise in the next session of that week only if the session stays at or under 45 min; do not push any group above 9 sets.
- Session time: if a Mon/Wed session runs over 45 min (planned 43.6 max; 32.8 in weeks 1 and 10), check that rest is 45 s after the main lifts and 30 s after band and accessory work, and do not lengthen the Bird Dog (5 per side) or knee activation (10 reps per leg). Do not drop the cardio block; shorten the session by trimming the warm-up march to 2 min only.
- One-variable rule (running): weeks 1-2 are walk-only and only the Saturday walk grows (20 to 25 min). Week 3 is the first jogging week and sets the start pattern (block length and repeats appear together). From week 4, only one variable changes per day per week: Mon/Wed jog block 1 to 2 min with repeats unchanged, Saturday block 2 to 3 min with repeats unchanged. From week 5 the weekly jog total rises 10% or less (33, 36, 39, 42, 46, 50 min); the 10% rule does not apply to weeks 3-4. Do not combine a longer jog block with more repeats on the same day, and do not add a new strength stressor in the week of the first jogging (week 3) or the long run (week 9).
- Run progression stalls: if the planned jog time for a week cannot be finished at pain 2/10 or less, repeat that week once; if it is repeated twice, step back one week. A repeat or step-back week resets the 10 percent baseline (from week 5 on) to that week's lower jog total. For the week 3-4 interval phase, repeat the whole week (Mon, Wed and Sat). The week 9 pace check below 4.2 km in 42 min means repeating week 9, which moves the goal run to the next Saturday after the program (the 5 km goal is about stamina, not speed).
- Missed sessions: if one session is missed, do it the next day only if it is not before a run day, otherwise skip it and carry on; if 2 or more sessions in a week are missed, repeat the week. After 7 or more days off, restart from the previous week's plan.
- Fat loss stalls: if the 3-week average of body weight is not lower than the previous 3-week average AND waist has not decreased, review the food and sleep log with the nutrition guidance first. Do not add training days, running volume or session length to compensate. If the weight is going down but waist and measurements are the same, keep going and recheck in 3 weeks (weight, waist and strength can change at different speeds).
- Rapid loss or fatigue: if she feels dizzy, is unusually tired for more than a week, or loses weight faster than she is comfortable with, go back to her doctor before continuing, and do not cut food further.
- Checkpoint fails: if a week 5 criterion is missed (walk, chair stand, plank), keep the plan, add the retest to week 7 on a Tuesday or Thursday, and use the rules above to find the cause (pain, load, sleep, food). If the week 5 Saturday run is not completed in full (21 jog min, longest jog 8 min) at pain 2/10 or less, repeat week 4 then week 5. If the week 3 safety check fails, repeat week 3. If the week 10 goal run is not met, schedule it again on the next Saturday after a lighter week; a repeat is acceptable.
