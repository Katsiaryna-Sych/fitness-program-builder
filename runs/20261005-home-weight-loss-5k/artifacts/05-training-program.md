# Training Program
> Run: 20261005-home-weight-loss-5k · Step: program · Owner: program-designer · Attempt: 5

## Summary
This is a 10-week, 3-day home program for a 52-year-old beginner (84 kg). Her main goal is fat loss, and she also wants to run 5 km without stopping. She has mild patellofemoral knee pain and her doctor has cleared her to exercise. The split is two full-body strength days and one cardio-led day. Mon (Session A) and Wed (Session B) each have 7 dumbbell, band and bodyweight strength exercises (21.6 min at full volume), then the cardio block from the cardio plan. Sat (Session C) is the cardio-led day. In weeks 1-2 it is a knee-activation drill plus a brisk walk. In weeks 3-6 it is the knee-activation drill plus a run/walk. In weeks 7-10 it is a continuous run only.

Changes in this version:
- Client feedback (still applied): weeks 1-2 are walk-only on every day, with no jog intervals. Jogging starts in week 3. Each strength day has one more upper-body exercise: One-Arm Dumbbell Row (2642) on Session A and Push-Up (1551) on Session B. Weekly push and pull sets stay equal (6:6 at full volume).
- Session time is now worked out from reps × seconds per rep at the top of each rep range, instead of a flat 45 s per set. Every set's time basis is shown in the Sessions tables and the duration arithmetic.
- To keep every Mon/Wed session at 44 min or less: rest after the four main lifts is cut from 60 s to 45 s; rest after the band rows, pull-aparts, monster walks and push-ups is cut from 45 s to 30 s; Bird Dog is 5 reps per side (was 6). The longest Mon/Wed session is now 43.6 min.
- From week 8, an exercise that reaches the 10 kg dumbbell limit (or the strongest band) stays at the top of its rep range with the same tempo. There are no extra reps and no slower lowering, so no set gets longer than the times shown.
- The weekly volume target is one range everywhere: 4-9 hard sets per muscle group per week.

Resistance training is there to keep lean mass while she loses fat. Most of the energy use comes from the aerobic work plus optional brisk walks on rest days. The program follows ACSM guidance for weight loss (resistance work keeps fat-free mass; 150-250+ min/week of moderate activity) and for novices (8-12 RM, 2-3 days/week, 2-10% load steps, rest under 90 s for endurance-type work).

Sets build from 1 per exercise in week 1 to 2 per exercise from week 2, while the cardio is still walk-only. This keeps the new strength volume (week 2) and the first jogging (week 3) in separate weeks. The RPE cap is 7 up to week 5 and 8 from week 6. Week 10 is a strength deload and the taper before the 5 km. Every Mon/Wed session is 43.6 min or less, including a 1-min transition from the strength area to the cardio block. Every Sat session in weeks 1-8 is 44 min or less. The only exceptions are the client-approved long runs on Day C in week 9 (52 min) and week 10 (60 min). The cardio blocks are copied exactly from the cardio plan.

Safety rules applied throughout:
- No jumping, no full-depth loaded knee bends and no step work.
- No kneeling directly on the kneecap without a mat.
- Every knee-loading exercise follows the 0-3/10 pain rule (never above 5/10).
- Running pain must stay at 2/10 or less.

Rationale:
- Evidence: Schoenfeld et al. (2017) found a graded dose-response, with each added weekly set giving about +0.37% more hypertrophy. Their weekly volume categories were <5, 5-9 and 10+ sets per muscle. ACSM (2009) recommends 8-12 RM loads and 2-3 days/week for novices.
- Why the low-to-moderate band (4-9 sets, spanning the <5 and 5-9 categories and staying below 10+): this client is a 52-year-old beginner with a fat-loss goal, a 45-min limit and a rising running load. The safety rule is "start with 1 set and progress to 2-3". The aim is to keep lean mass during weight loss (ACSM 2009 weight-loss position stand; EASO), not to maximise hypertrophy.
- The added upper-body exercises raise push and pull from 4 to 6 sets per week each, still within 4-9 and balanced 6:6 (Session A has 1 push : 2 pull and Session B has 2 push : 1 pull, so the week is equal).

## Progression Plan
| week | change | target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction: 1 set of every strength exercise (7 per session), 10-12 reps (bands 12-15). Choose loads that feel RPE 5-6: bodyweight box squat, lunge and incline push-up (counter height); 3-6 kg dumbbells. Cardio is walk-only: A/B brisk walk 15 min, C brisk walk 20 min (jog total 0) | Learn technique and the tempos; strength pain 0-3/10; nothing worse the next morning |
| 2 | +1 set on every exercise (A1-A7, B1-B7 to 2 sets; full volume). Same loads, RPE ≤ 7. Cardio is still walk-only, and only the Day C walk grows (20 to 25 min). The new resistance volume is the week's only new stressor, because walking is her baseline activity | 2 × 10-12 on every exercise at RPE ≤ 7 with good form |
| 3 | Strength held (same sets and loads, RPE ≤ 7). First jogging week, which is the single new stressor: A/B 3 x 1 min jog (13-min block), C 6 x 2 min jog (jog total 18 min, longest 2) | Every jog interval at zone 2 with running pain ≤ 2/10 |
| 4 | Double progression begins for the upper body only (floor press, one-arm row, band row, shoulder press, pull-aparts; push-up by lowering the hand height). When every set reaches the top of the rep range at RPE ≤ 7 in two sessions in a row, add about 2-10% (the next dumbbell, about +1 kg per hand, or a stronger band) and drop back to the bottom of the range. Lower-body loads are held. Cardio: one variable per day (A/B jog block 1 to 2 min; C jog block 2 to 3 min; repeats unchanged; jog total 30) | Upper-body reps at the top of the range; RPE ≤ 7 |
| 5 | Continuous-running phase starts (Day C longest jog 8 min; jog total 33, +10.0%). Strength: only the upper-body double progression continues; lower-body loads, sets and the RPE cap of 7 are unchanged (one new stressor at a time) | Same lower-body loads with reps at 12 at RPE ≤ 7 |
| 6 | RPE cap rises to 8. Lower-body load step if earned: hold dumbbells at the sides for box squat and reverse lunges (start at 3 kg each), or add about 1 kg per hand on the deadlift and RDL. Upper-body double progression continues. Running is not a second new stressor this week: the weekly jog total goes 33 to 36 min (+9.1%, within the 10% rule) and the Mon/Wed jog falls from 6 to 4 min (see the note below) | 2 × 10-12 at RPE 7-8 with knee pain 0-3/10 |
| 7 | Hold sets and lower-body loads (the long jog grows to 26 min; jog total 39, +8.3%). Upper-body double progression continues | Reps back to the top of the range at the week 6 loads; RPE ≤ 8 |
| 8 | Second lower-body load step if earned (+1 kg per hand, up to the 10 kg maximum). Any exercise that has reached 10 kg (or the strongest band) is held at the top of its rep range with the same tempo; there are no extra reps and no slower lowering, so no set gets longer than the times in Sessions. Running is not a second new stressor: the weekly jog total goes 39 to 42 min (+7.7%) and the Mon/Wed blocks are unchanged (see the note below) | 2 × 10-12 (bands 12-15) at RPE ≤ 8; knee pain 0-3/10 |
| 9 | Hold the same sets, reps, tempo and loads as week 8. There is no new resistance stressor, because the 42 min long run happens this week (safety rule). Day C uses the approved 52-min exception | Completed at RPE ≤ 8 with running pain ≤ 2/10; week 9 pace check of about 4.2 km in 42 min |
| 10 | Deload and taper: 1 set per exercise at the week 8-9 loads, RPE 5-6 (strength 10.8 min). The A/B cardio blocks are 15 min easy brisk walks. Day C is the 5 km goal run (≤ 60 min) | Run 5 km without stopping on Saturday; fresh legs; pain ≤ 2/10 |

Repeat rule (any week): if strength pain goes above 5/10, running pain goes above 2/10, or pain is worse the next morning, repeat the previous week's loads and cardio week instead of progressing. Repeating weeks is acceptable (NHS Couch to 5K).

Set-length rule (all weeks): reps never go above the top of each rep range and the tempos never get slower, so the per-set times used in the duration arithmetic are the maximum in every week.

Note on weeks 6 and 8 (a load step in the same week as a longer longest jog):
- The safety guidelines define a new stressor as new impact or new load, and they measure running load as total weekly jog minutes. From week 5, the first continuous week, that total may rise by up to 10%.
- Continuous running starts in week 5, and strength is held that week. After that, the longer Saturday jog is the planned continuation of the same stimulus, kept within the 10% rule (+9.1% in week 6, +7.7% in week 8). It is not a new type of impact.
- The load step falls on Mon/Wed. On those days the jog volume drops (6 to 4 min in week 6) or stays the same (week 8). Thursday and Friday rest separate the load step from the Saturday run.
- The load step could not move to a week without a longer longest jog: every week from 5 to 9 extends it, and weeks 5, 7 and 9 already hold the lower-body load.
- The load step is conditional. Skip it and keep the previous loads if any run in the previous week reached more than 2/10 knee pain or the knee was worse the next morning.

## Deload & Recovery
- Rest days: there is at least one rest day between all training days (Mon, Wed, Sat), and no running on consecutive days. Tue, Thu and Sun are optional active recovery: a 20-30 min zone 2 brisk walk, from week 5 or earlier if the knees feel fine. These walks bring weekly moderate activity toward 150 min or more (ACSM weight-loss position stand: 150-250 min/week gives modest loss, and more than 250 min/week is linked with clinically significant loss). Friday is full rest before the Saturday session.
- Deload timing: week 10 is a planned deload (1 set per exercise at RPE 5-6) and also the taper for the 5 km run. Week 9 holds the strength load so the long run is the only new stressor. Weeks 1-2 are walk-only and week 3 holds strength, so the first jogging is not combined with new resistance volume. An extra, unplanned deload (repeat the previous week with 1 set per exercise) is used whenever the repeat rule above is triggered.
- Knee monitoring: check knee pain during and after every session. During strength work, aim for 0-3/10 and never go above 5/10. During running, keep it at 2/10 or less. Pain should settle within 30 min and be no worse the next morning. See the doctor or a physiotherapist if pain does not improve after 6 weeks of consistent exercise, or if swelling, locking or giving way appears.
- Stop the session and seek medical advice for chest pain or pressure, dizziness, fainting, palpitations or severe breathlessness.
- Sleep and recovery: aim for 7 or more hours of sleep. Recovery days are as much a part of the plan as training days; the NHS describes rest days as "just as important as the runs themselves". Nutrition guidance is provided separately.
