# Cardio Plan
> Run: 20261005-home-weight-loss-5k · Step: cardio · Owner: cardio-planner · Attempt: 3

## Summary
A 10-week walk-to-run progression for a 52-year-old beginner (84 kg) with mild patellofemoral knee pain who wants to lose fat and run 5 km without stopping. Cardio is 3 blocks per week on the 3 training days, rising from 50 to 90 minutes per week. Weeks 1-2 are brisk walking only, with no jogging on any day, to prepare the knees and build the habit. Jogging starts in week 3 with short run-walk intervals, then builds to a continuous run. The NHS Couch to 5K run-walk idea is slowed down and stretched over 10 weeks. Optional brisk walks on rest days (outside the session limit) bring total weekly aerobic activity to the 150-minute target from about week 5-6. No equipment is needed. Run on flat, soft surfaces where possible. Running pain must stay at 2/10 or less; above that, repeat or step back a week.

Phases, matching the safety guidelines:
- Walk-only phase, weeks 1-2: brisk walking on Day A, Day B and Day C. No jog intervals.
- Interval phase, weeks 3-4: every jog block is 5 min or less (longest block 3 min). Week 3 is the first jogging week, so it sets the starting pattern (a jog block and a number of repeats have to appear together for the first time). In week 4 only ONE variable changes on each training day (jog block length; repeats stay the same). The 10 % rule does not apply here.
- Continuous phase, weeks 5-10: week 5 is the first week in which a jog block exceeds 5 min (8 min on Day C), so it is the first continuous week. Every week from 5 has total jog minutes at 110 % or less of the previous week (week 5 is exactly +10 %).

Client decision (Round 3): the week-9 and week-10 Day C long runs may last up to about 60 min = 5 min walking warm-up + up to about 50 min continuous jog + 5 min walking cool-down (the same 5-min walks as the NHS plan). Every other session stays at 45 min or less.

Week-10 time math (Day C):
- 5 km at 9.0 min/km = 45 min; 9.5 = 47.5 min; 10.0 = 50 min.
- Planned session: 5 min walking warm-up + 50 min continuous jog + 5 min walking cool-down = 60 min (the approved maximum).
- Pace needed in the final week, stated plainly: the 5 km must be covered at an average of 10 min/km (6 km/h) or faster. At exactly 10 min/km, 50 min of jogging is 5.0 km. A faster pace finishes the 5 km earlier and the session is shorter (for example 9.0 min/km = 45 min jog, 55 min total). Slower than 10 min/km means 50 min will not reach 5 km: repeat week 10 (or week 9) rather than extending beyond 50 min of jogging.
- Pace check in week 9: the 42 min continuous jog should cover about 4.2 km or more (10 min/km or faster). If it covers less, repeat week 9 before attempting week 10.

Why the cap works: weekly jog minutes (Day A + B + C) run 30, 33, 36, 39, 42, 46, 50 from week 4 to week 10, every step 10 % or less. Week 10 is 8.7 % above week 9. Making weeks 1-2 walk-only leaves two interval weeks (3 and 4) to reach the week-5 level, so the weeks 3-4 jogging is kept as high as the one-variable rule and the 15-min block limit allow, and the week 4 to week 5 step is exactly +10 %.

## Cardio Goal
- Primary: reach at least 150 min/week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity (WHO range 150-300 min) by combining the 3 cardio blocks with 2-3 extra brisk walks; muscle strengthening on 2 or more days is covered by the strength program. Source: WHO 2020 guidelines.
- Event goal: run 5 km continuously by week 10. Structure adapted from the NHS Couch to 5K plan (3 sessions per week, run-walk intervals building to a continuous run, 5 min warm-up and cool-down walks, repeating weeks is fine). Source: NHS Couch to 5K.
- Repeat a week if it feels too hard or running pain is above 2/10; do not skip ahead (NHS advice, safety guidelines).
- Safety alignment: running pain 2/10 or less; weeks 1-2 walk-only; in weeks 3-4 (all jog blocks 5 min or less) change one variable per day per week after the first jogging week; total weekly jog minutes (Day A + B + C) rise 10 % or less per week from week 5 (the first continuous week) to week 10; no consecutive running days; Day A and B cardio blocks 15 min or less so strength (7 exercises) + cardio stay within 45 min; every session except the week-9 and week-10 Day C runs is 45 min or less.

## Intensity Zones
Estimated maximum heart rate = 220 - 52 = 168 bpm. These are averages and only a guide.

| zone | description | heart rate / RPE / talk test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Easy | Warm-up, cool-down and recovery walking | 50-60 % ≈ 84-100 bpm, RPE 2-3/10, can sing or chat freely |
| 2 Moderate (main zone) | Brisk walking and easy jogging; steady effort | 50-70 % ≈ 84-118 bpm, RPE 4-5/10, can talk in full sentences |
| 3 Vigorous (limit) | Hard jogging; avoid in weeks 1-8 and keep brief at most in weeks 9-10 | 70-85 % ≈ 118-143 bpm, RPE 6-7/10, only short phrases. Slow down if you reach this zone |

Brisk walking in weeks 1-2 should feel like zone 2 (a little breathless but able to talk in full sentences). Jog intervals should stay at zone 2 (talk test); a 9-10 min/km jog is slow enough for this. If you cannot speak a full sentence, slow down or walk. Heart rate may drift into the low end of zone 3 during the long jogs in weeks 9-10; slow down rather than push.

Jog totals per week (min): 0, 0, 18, 30, 33, 36, 39, 42, 46, 50. Weeks 1-2 are walk-only. Weeks 3-4 are the interval phase (blocks of 5 min or less; week 4 changes one variable per day). Week 5 is the first continuous week; every step from week 5 is 10 % or less (33/30 = 1.10, 36/33 = 1.09, 39/36 = 1.08, 42/39 = 1.08, 46/42 = 1.095, 50/46 = 1.087).

Longest continuous jog per week (min): 0, 0, 2, 3, 8, 18, 26, 34, 42, 50. Jog minutes are gradually moved from Days A and B to Day C so the long jog can grow within the weekly cap.

Weekly minutes (walk + jog): 50, 55, 57, 67, 63, 70, 73, 74, 82, 90. Week 5 is 4 min lower than week 4 because the walking bookends shrink while the longer jog blocks begin.

Session lengths: every Day A and B cardio block is 15 min or less in every week (4 min warm-up + about 23.5 min strength + block + 2 min cool-down stays at 45 min or less). Day C is 45 min or less in weeks 1-8 (max 44). Day C is 52 min (week 9) and 60 min (week 10), the client-approved exception.

Extra non-session walking: 2-3 brisk walks of 20-30 min on rest days (zone 2), from week 5 as needed to reach 150 min/week of moderate activity (optional earlier if the knees feel fine). These do not add jog minutes.

## Integration With Strength Training
- Train 3 days per week with at least one rest day between sessions (for example Mon / Wed / Sat). Do not run on consecutive days. Day C should be the day furthest from the heaviest lower-body strength work.
- Order on Days A and B: strength first (about 23.5 min, 7 exercises), then the cardio block (15 min or less) so that the legs are fresh for lifting. 4 min warm-up + about 23.5 min strength + 15 min block + 2 min cool-down = 44.5 min, within the 45-minute limit.
- Day C weeks 1-8: cardio-led block of 20-44 min, total 45 minutes or less including at most a short mobility finisher.
- Day C weeks 9-10: cardio only, 52 min (week 9) and 60 min (week 10), each 5 min walking warm-up + continuous jog + 5 min walking cool-down. This is the client-approved exception of up to about 60 min. No strength work on Day C in these two weeks.
- Week 10: Days A and B keep their strength work with only a 15 min easy brisk walk, so legs are fresh for the 5 km.
- Knee protection: jog on flat soft surfaces, avoid downhill running and deep knee flexion right before running. Running pain must stay at 2/10 or less. If pain goes above 2/10 or lasts after the session, replace the jog with a walk, repeat or step back a week, and consult the safety guidelines or her doctor.
- Stop and seek medical advice for chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness.
