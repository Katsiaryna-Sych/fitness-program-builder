# Nutrition & Recovery
> Run: 20261005-home-weight-loss-5k · Step: nutrition · Owner: nutrition-planner · Attempt: 4

## Summary
The goal is fat loss, so the direction is a moderate energy deficit of about 300-500 kcal per day below estimated maintenance. For a 52-year-old woman (168 cm, 84 kg) this works out to roughly 1,550-1,700 kcal per day (about 1,600 kcal as a working number). Protein is set at 1.4-1.6 g/kg, which is 118-134 g per day, to help keep muscle while she loses fat. The key habits are three protein-containing meals plus one or two snacks, a light carbohydrate-containing snack before the Monday, Wednesday and Saturday sessions, fluids spread through the day, and 7 or more hours of sleep. Weight is expected to fall slowly (around 0.3-0.5 kg per week). Weeks 1-2 are walk-only, and jogging starts in week 3. Monday and Wednesday sessions last up to 43.6 min (about 21.6 min of strength work from week 2, with short rests of 45 s after the main lifts and 30 s after band and accessory work). Weekly cardio minutes grow from 50 in week 1 to 90 in week 10 (50, 55, 57, 67, 63, 70, 73, 74, 82, 90), so intake should not be cut further than the target. The activity factor for the energy estimate was checked against the updated session lengths and stays the same, so the calculations are unchanged. All numbers are estimates from a prediction formula and should be adjusted after 2-3 weeks based on real weight trends.

## Meal Timing
- Daily pattern: 3 meals plus 1-2 snacks, each with about 25-35 g of protein at meals (eggs, Greek yoghurt, cottage cheese, fish, poultry, lean meat, tofu, pulses) and about 10-20 g in snacks. The ISSN stand suggests spreading protein evenly through the day, roughly every 3-4 hours, rather than eating most of it at dinner.
- Training days (Mon = Session A, Wed = Session B, Sat = Session C): eat a balanced meal 2-3 hours before, or a small carbohydrate-containing snack (a banana, toast with a thin spread, yoghurt with fruit) 30-60 minutes before if training is early. Avoid a heavy meal right before the cardio block so walking or jogging stays comfortable.
- Mon and Wed sessions are up to 43.6 min. They combine strength work (about 21.6 min from week 2, and about 10.8 min in weeks 1 and 10) with a 13-15 min cardio block. Rests are short (45 s after the main lifts and 30 s after band and accessory work), so there is little time to eat or drink during the session; have the pre-session snack beforehand. The cardio block is a brisk walk in weeks 1-2 and an easy walk in week 10, and a run/walk block in weeks 3-9. The longest Mon/Wed sessions (43.6 min) are in weeks 2 and 4-9, so a pre-session snack matters most then; week 3 is 41.6 min. In weeks 1 and 10 the strength work is shorter (32.8 min in total), so a small snack is enough.
- After training: a meal or snack with protein (20-40 g) and carbohydrate within a couple of hours. The ISSN stand notes total daily protein and energy matter most, and the post-exercise window is wide (at least 24 hours), so there is no need to rush.
- Saturday sessions (Session C) change over the program. Weeks 1-2 are a knee-activation drill plus a brisk walk (24 and 29 min). Weeks 3-6 are the drill plus a run/walk block (35, 41, 37 and 44 min). Weeks 7-10 are a continuous run only (43, 44, 52 and 60 min). From week 7, eat a carbohydrate-rich meal the evening before and a light breakfast or snack before the run. Do not try new foods on run days, and especially not before the 5 km run in week 10. Put more of the day's carbohydrate around this session rather than cutting calories further.
- Rest days (Tue, Thu, Fri, Sun): keep the same protein target, and keep total calories at the same target. Do not "make up" for training by eating less. Optional brisk walks on Tue, Thu and Sun are covered by the same target; do not eat less to offset them.
- Pre-sleep: if hungry in the evening, a protein-containing snack (for example Greek yoghurt or cottage cheese) is a reasonable option; the ISSN stand reports 30-40 g of casein before sleep supports overnight muscle protein synthesis, but a smaller portion fits her calorie budget.
- Consistency matters more than perfection: the NHS advises splitting a calorie reduction across the day (for example small cuts at each meal and snacks) instead of one large cut.

## Hydration
- Daily: the NHS advises about 6-8 cups or glasses of fluid a day (roughly 1.5-2 litres). Water, lower-fat milk, tea and coffee all count, and food provides some fluid. Aim for a clear pale yellow urine colour as a simple check.
- Training days: start sessions already hydrated and keep a water bottle at the strength area. Take small sips in the 30-45 s rests between strength sets and during the walking parts of the cardio blocks. Water is the best fluid for replacing sweat in sessions of this length. Add extra fluid on warm days and for the long Saturday runs in weeks 9-10 (52 and 60 minutes).
- Limit sugary drinks and keep fruit juice or smoothies to a small glass (150 ml) with a meal at most, since they add calories without filling her up (NHS).
- If she has heart, kidney or other medical conditions that affect fluid advice, she should follow her doctor's guidance.

## Sleep & Recovery
- Sleep: aim for 7 or more hours per night (CDC recommendation for adults aged 18-60). Short sleep is linked with obesity and weight gain among other risks, and it makes recovery from strength work and running harder.
- Rest days: keep Tue, Thu, Fri and Sun for recovery or the optional easy walks in the program. Friday is a full rest day before the Saturday session, so avoid heavy activity then.
- Muscle and joint recovery: the program starts with two walk-only weeks, adds jogging only in week 3, and uses a deload in week 10 (shorter strength work and an easy walk on Mon and Wed before the 5 km run). If the knee is sore the next morning, repeat the previous week as the program says, rather than cutting food further.
- Stress and routine: keep consistent meal and sleep times, plan meals in advance for the three training days, and use a wind-down routine in the evening (screens off, low light). Weigh once or twice a week at the same time of day and judge the trend over 2-3 weeks, not single readings.
- Warning signs: persistent fatigue, dizziness, feeling cold all the time, poor sleep or unusual bleeding mean the deficit may be too large or something else is going on. Eat at maintenance for a few days and speak to a doctor.

## Disclaimer
- General guidance only, not medical or dietetic advice. She has a diagnosed knee condition; any other medical condition (diabetes, heart, kidney, thyroid, eating-disorder history), pregnancy or medication should be discussed with her doctor or a registered dietitian before she follows a calorie target.
