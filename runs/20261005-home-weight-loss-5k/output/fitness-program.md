# 10-Week Home Fat-Loss and First 5 km Program

## Program Overview
| item | value |
|---|---|
| goal | Lose fat (main goal) and run 5 km without stopping by the end of week 10 |
| level | Beginner (regular walking only so far) |
| schedule | 3 days/week (Mon, Wed, Sat), 45 min or less per session. One exception you agreed to: the long runs on Saturday in week 9 (52 min) and week 10 (up to 60 min) |
| length | 10 weeks (week 10 is a lighter strength week before your 5 km run) |
| location & equipment | At home. Dumbbells up to 10 kg, resistance bands, plus a sturdy chair, a mat and a kitchen counter or table edge |

How the program fits together:
- Monday and Wednesday are full-body strength sessions (7 exercises each) followed by a cardio block. Strength work takes about 21.6 minutes at full volume, and the whole session takes 43.6 minutes at most.
- Saturday is your cardio-led day: a knee-activation drill plus walking or run/walk early on, then a continuous run from week 7.
- Weeks 1-2 are walk-only, with no running intervals on any day. Jogging starts in week 3.
- Strength training here is to protect muscle while you lose fat. Most of the energy use comes from the cardio and the optional brisk walks on rest days.

## Safety Notes
Overall risk is moderate and manageable when you let pain guide your effort. This plan adjusts knee-loading movements rather than removing them, and it is not medical advice.

**Pain rules**
- Strength work: aim for pain of 0-3/10 and never above 5/10. If pain rises, reduce the range, reps, load or speed. Pain should settle within 30 minutes and be no worse the next morning.
- Running: keep pain at 2/10 or less. If it goes higher, walk instead and repeat or step back a week.
- Long runs in weeks 9-10: if pain reaches 3/10, walk for 2-3 minutes and carry on jogging only if it drops to 2/10 or less. Otherwise finish the session walking.

**Movements to avoid**
| movement | why | what to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Jumping of any kind (jump squats, jumping lunges, skipping, burpees with a jump) | High impact loads the front of the knee | Box squat to a chair, glute bridge, banded walks, marching |
| Deep loaded knee bends (full-depth squats or lunges, sissy squats, deep split squats) taken into pain above 5/10 | Squatting and stairs are the main aggravators | Partial-range squat to a chair, reverse lunge in a pain-free range, slow 3-second lowering |
| Fast running build-ups: continuous running from week 1, big jumps in running time, repeated sprints, steep hills | Rapid increases are linked with more running injuries | Walk first, then run/walk on flat ground, then longer jogs as set out in this plan |
| Fast or high step-ups and step-downs, and any exercise that hurts while you do it | Stairs and steps are common triggers | Skip step work, or use a low step with slow tempo and wall support |
| Near-maximal lifting (RPE above 8, 1-5 rep max) | Beginners should start light to protect joints | Stay at RPE 7 or less in weeks 1-5 and 8 or less afterwards |

**Running rules for this program**
- Run on flat ground, never on two days in a row, and use an easy, talk-in-sentences effort.
- Weeks 1-2 are walk-only and jogging starts in week 3. You start jogging only if the week 1-2 walks were done at knee pain 2/10 or less and no worse the next morning.
- In weeks 3-4 the jog blocks are all 5 minutes or less. After the first jogging week (week 3), change only one thing per day each week (a longer jog block or more repeats, never both). This is a conservative rule for this program.
- From week 5, when jog blocks first go above 5 minutes, your total weekly jogging time rises by no more than 10% a week (walking is not counted).
- A higher step rate (about 7.5-10% more steps per minute) can reduce knee load. Treat it as optional.
- Do not add more than one new stress (new impact or new load) in the same week.

**Stop and get medical advice** for chest pain or pressure, severe breathlessness, dizziness or fainting, or palpitations. Also get advice if you have knee swelling, locking, giving way or night pain, if pain is worse the next morning or does not return to baseline within 30 minutes, or if there is no improvement after 6 weeks of consistent exercise. Also seek advice for trauma, fever, a hot swollen joint or calf swelling. Confirm your doctor's clearance before you start jogging, and mention the long runs planned for weeks 9-10.

## Cardio & Conditioning
**Goals:** build to at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity (the 3 session blocks plus optional brisk walks) and run 5 km without stopping by week 10. The structure is adapted from the NHS Couch to 5K plan, slowed down and spread over 10 weeks. Repeating a week is fine.

**Phases**
- Weeks 1-2, walk-only: brisk walking on Monday, Wednesday and Saturday. No jogging at all.
- Weeks 3-4, interval phase: jogging starts in week 3. Every jog block is 5 minutes or less (longest 3). The 10% weekly rule does not apply yet; the one-change-per-day rule from Safety Notes does, from week 4.
- Weeks 5-10, continuous phase: week 5 is the first week with a jog block above 5 minutes. From here, total jog minutes rise by 10% or less each week.

| protein | 1.4-1.6 g/kg = 118-134 g/day (about 126 g) | 84 kg x 1.4 = 118 g; 84 kg x 1.6 = 134 g. Spread over 4 eating occasions, about 25-35 g at meals and 10-20 g in snacks |
| carbohydrate / fat | Carbohydrate about 150 g (about 38%); fat about 55 g (about 31%); protein about 126 g (about 31%) on a 1,600 kcal day | A practical split after protein is set, not a cited requirement. Scale proportionally within 1,550-1,700 kcal. Favour whole grains, fruit, vegetables, pulses and unsaturated fats |

No food preferences or dietary restrictions were given, so this is general guidance. A measured metabolic rate or body fat percentage would make it more precise.

**Meal timing**
- Eat 3 meals plus 1-2 snacks, with protein at each (eggs, Greek yoghurt, cottage cheese, fish, poultry, lean meat, tofu, pulses). Spread protein roughly every 3-4 hours rather than eating most of it at dinner.
- Training days (Mon, Wed, Sat): eat a balanced meal 2-3 hours before, or a small carbohydrate snack 30-60 minutes before if you train early (a banana, toast with a thin spread, yoghurt with fruit). Avoid a heavy meal right before the cardio block.
- Mon and Wed sessions last up to 43.6 min with short rests, so there is little time to eat during them. Have your snack beforehand; it matters most in weeks 2 and 4-9, the longest sessions.
- Saturday changes over the program. Weeks 1-2 are the drill plus a walk, weeks 3-6 the drill plus run/walk, and weeks 7-10 a continuous run of 43, 44, 52 and up to 60 min. From week 7, eat a carbohydrate-rich meal the evening before and a light breakfast or snack before the run. Do not try new foods on run days, especially before the 5 km run in week 10.
- After training, have a meal or snack with 20-40 g of protein and some carbohydrate within a couple of hours. There is no need to rush it.
- On rest days (Tue, Thu, Fri, Sun) keep the same protein and calorie target. Do not eat less to make up for training, and do not eat less to offset the optional walks.
- If you are hungry in the evening, a protein snack such as Greek yoghurt or cottage cheese is reasonable.
- Split any calorie reduction across the day (small cuts at each meal and snack) instead of one large cut.

**Hydration**
- Aim for about 6-8 glasses or cups of fluid a day (roughly 1.5-2 litres). Water, lower-fat milk, tea and coffee all count. Pale yellow urine is a simple check.
- Start sessions well hydrated, keep a bottle at your strength area and take small sips in the 30-45 second rests and during the walking parts. Drink extra on warm days and for the long Saturday runs in weeks 9-10.
- Limit sugary drinks and keep juice or smoothies to a small glass (150 ml) with a meal at most.
- If you have heart, kidney or other conditions that affect fluid advice, follow your doctor's guidance.

**Sleep and recovery**
- Aim for 7 or more hours of sleep a night. Short sleep makes recovery harder and is linked with weight gain.
- There is at least one rest day between all training days and you never run on consecutive days. Tue, Thu and Sun can be optional 20-30 minute zone 2 brisk walks. Friday is a full rest day before Saturday. Rest days are just as important as the runs themselves.
- Week 10 is a planned deload and taper. If your knee is sore the next morning, repeat the previous week as the plan says rather than cutting food further.
- Keep consistent meal and sleep times, plan meals for the three training days, and use a wind-down routine in the evening. Weigh once or twice a week at the same time of day and judge the trend over 2-3 weeks, not single readings.
- Warning signs: persistent fatigue, dizziness, feeling cold all the time, poor sleep or unusual bleeding may mean the deficit is too large or something else is going on. Eat at maintenance for a few days and speak to a doctor.

## Tracking & Assessments
All tests are done at home with a tape measure, a chair, a timer and a flat route. No test is maximal and running is never tested for speed. Do tests on the Tuesday and Thursday rest days or before a session so no training session goes over its time limit.

**Baseline tests (week 1, repeated in weeks 5 and 10)**
| test | how to do it | what it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Waist circumference and body weight | In the morning before eating, light clothing, feet together, arms at your sides. Tape level, snug but not pressing, just above the top of the right hip bone. Read at the end of a normal breath out, to 0.1 cm. Measure twice and record the average. Weigh on the same scale at the same time. This is a separate morning measurement and does not count toward session time. Reference line: above 88 cm for women is the abdominal obesity threshold used with this method | Fat loss (main goal) |
| 6-minute walk test | Tuesday. Flat, hard path of 30 m marked with two markers, or a measured out-and-back route. Walk as fast as comfortable for exactly 6 minutes (no running). Rests are allowed and the clock keeps running. Record total distance, start and end knee pain and RPE. Stop for chest pain, dizziness, severe breathlessness or knee pain above 5/10 | Aerobic base |
| 30-second chair stand | Thursday. Straight-back armless chair, seat about 43 cm high, against a wall. Arms crossed on your chest. Stand fully and sit as many times as you can in 30 seconds at a steady pace. Count a stand if you are more than halfway up when time ends. Use a cushion if pain rises and stop at once if knee pain reaches 4/10. If you must use your arms, record 0 | Lower-body strength and endurance |
| Forearm plank hold (capped) | Thursday, after the chair stand and a 3-minute rest. Forearms on a mat, body straight from head to heels or head to knees (keep the same version every time). Stop when your hips sag or at 60 seconds. If kneeling hurts, use the toes version or a wall-supported plank | Core endurance |
| Saturday cardio session | Done in your normal Saturday session. Week 1: a 20-minute brisk walk, a walk-only baseline (record distance, pace, RPE, knee pain). Week 5: 5 min walk, 8 min jog, 2 min walk, 8 min jog, 2 min walk, 5 min jog, 3 min walk (21 jog min, longest jog 8). Week 10: the 5 km goal run. Pain of 3/10 means walk 2-3 minutes and resume only once it falls to 2/10 or less | Walking base, first run capacity, then the 5 km goal |

**Adjustment rules**
- Walking pain, weeks 1-2: if walking pain is above 3/10, or the knee is worse the next morning, repeat that week before going on. Do not start jogging in week 3 unless the weeks 1-2 walks were done at 2/10 or less and no worse the next morning.
- Pain of exactly 3/10 while walking (weeks 1-2 and the Mon/Wed walks in week 10): slow to an easy pace for 2-3 minutes and keep going only if it falls to 2/10 or less. Otherwise finish very easily and add no time. The session counts as done, but the week is a hold: do not progress and repeat that week once. If the same week reaches 3/10 twice, stop progressing and see your doctor or physiotherapist.
- Running pain: if it is above 2/10, or the knee is worse the next morning, repeat the previous week's run plan and keep strength loads unchanged. If it reaches 3/10 during a jog, walk 2-3 minutes and resume only if it falls to 2/10 or less; otherwise finish walking.
- Strength pain: if pain goes above 5/10 in any exercise, stop it, switch to the easier option (for example swap reverse lunges for box squat, or use a higher hand position for the push-up) and repeat the previous week's loads. If pain is still above 3/10 after 2 weeks, or does not improve after 6 weeks, or there is swelling, locking or giving way, stop progressing and see your doctor or physiotherapist.
- Test pain: if the chair stand or plank causes knee pain above 3/10, record the score where you stopped, use the cushion or toes version, and keep that version for every later checkpoint.
- Too hard: if a session is rated RPE 8 or higher in weeks 1-5, or RPE 9 or higher in weeks 6-9, repeat the previous week's loads. Two such sessions in a row mean the next week is a repeat week with 1 set per exercise.
- Too easy: if all sets are finished at the top of the rep range at RPE 5 or less in two sessions in a row, take the next load step (about 2-10%, about 1 kg per hand, a stronger band or a lower hand height for the push-up). From week 4 this applies to the upper body only; lower-body steps start from week 6. Do not add extra sessions, repeats, longer jog blocks or running minutes beyond the plan.
- At the limit (weeks 8-9): an exercise already at 10 kg per hand or the strongest band stays at the top of its rep range with the same tempo. Do not add reps, slow the lowering or add sets, because Mon/Wed sessions are already up to 43.6 minutes. Log it as "at limit".
- Weekly volume: if a muscle group counts below 4 hard sets in weeks 2-9, repeat the missed exercise in the next session of that week only if the session stays at or under 45 min. Never push any group above 9 sets.
- Session time: if a Mon/Wed session runs over 45 minutes, check that your rests are 45 s after the main lifts and 30 s after band and accessory work, and do not lengthen the Bird Dog (5 per side) or the knee activation (10 reps per leg). Keep the cardio block and shorten the warm-up march to 2 minutes if needed.
- Running rule for this program: weeks 1-2 are walk-only and only the Saturday walk grows. Week 3 is the first jogging week and sets the starting pattern. From week 4, change only one variable per day each week. From week 5, weekly jog total rises 10% or less. Do not add a new strength stress in week 3 (first jogging) or week 9 (the long run).
- Running stalls: if you cannot finish a week's jog time at pain 2/10 or less, repeat that week once; if you repeat it twice, step back one week. A repeat or step-back week resets the 10% baseline to that lower week. In weeks 3-4, repeat the whole week. If the week 9 pace check is not met, repeat week 9, which moves your goal run to the next Saturday after the program. The 5 km goal is about stamina, not speed.
- Missed sessions: if you miss one session, do it the next day only if that day is not before a run day; otherwise skip it and carry on. If you miss 2 or more sessions in a week, repeat the week. After 7 or more days off, restart from the previous week's plan.
- Fat-loss stalls: if your 3-week average weight is not lower than the previous 3-week average and your waist has not decreased, review your food and sleep first. Do not add training days, running or session length to compensate. If weight is falling but measurements are not, keep going and recheck in 3 weeks.
- Rapid loss or fatigue: if you feel dizzy, are unusually tired for more than a week, or lose weight faster than you are comfortable with, speak to your doctor before continuing and do not cut food further.
- Checkpoint fails: if a week 5 walk, chair stand or plank criterion is missed, keep the plan, add a retest in week 7 on a Tuesday or Thursday and look for the cause (pain, load, sleep, food). If the week 5 Saturday run is not completed in full at 2/10 or less, repeat week 4 then week 5. If the week 3 check fails, repeat week 3. If the week 10 goal run is not met, schedule it again on the next Saturday after a lighter week.

## Disclaimer
This program is general fitness guidance and not medical advice. You have a diagnosed knee condition, so check with your doctor or a physiotherapist before you start jogging, and raise any other condition (diabetes, heart, kidney or thyroid problems, a history of disordered eating), pregnancy or medication before following a calorie target. The nutrition figures are estimates from a prediction formula and are not exact. Stop any session and seek medical advice if you have chest pain, severe breathlessness, dizziness, fainting or palpitations.
